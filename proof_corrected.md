# Finite-Bar Discrete General Relativity: Lattice Wave Dynamics on a Planck-Density (Hayward) Black Hole — What Works, What Does Not, and What Is Still Open

## Abstract
We study discrete general relativity on a Planck-spacing lattice ($\ell = \ell_P$) with a density cap $\rho_{\max} = 3c^2/(8\pi G\ell^2)$, using (a) a scalar test field on prescribed Hayward and effective-LQC black-hole backgrounds, (b) spherically symmetric dust and Einstein–Euler collapse on co-moving shells, (c) random Voronoi–Delaunay and smoothly stretched ("anamorphic") grids, and (d) a second-order discretisation of the spherical Einstein constraints plus a linearised 3D audit of metric perturbations on the 18-point stencil. We find that the 18-point stencil is isotropic to $\mathcal{O}(\ell^4)$ and gives a tree-level GRB 090510 delay of $\sim10^{-19}$ s; a skew-symmetric Cayley scheme conserves energy and traps waves inside $r_+$; a cap on cell energy cannot prevent the singularity, whereas an imposed effective-LQC curvature cap bounces every shell at $R_{\text{core}} = (r_s\ell^2)^{1/3}$; the HKSW shock escapes the horizon only superluminally; relativistic shock pressure prevents shell crossing, but the quantitative outcome depends on a free dissipation length; a static random lattice would Rayleigh-scatter observed PeV photons ($\tau \approx 2$); the discretised Einstein/TOV constraints converge at $\mathcal{O}(1/N^2)$ to the Schwarzschild exterior and interior solutions without a metric ansatz (Einstein's equations remain input); and the 18-point bar network is the smallest cubic stencil that can represent all six strain components. The core cannot store the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy, and the saturation rule does not resolve mass inflation. No result here derives Einstein's equations from the lattice, and the model makes no unconditional observational prediction.

10. **The 3D "self-gravity" simulation (§4.4) is not GR.**

## 1. Curvature Breakdown in Continuous GR & The Planck-Scale UV Lattice Postulates

### 1.1 Where Continuous General Relativity Breaks ($r \to 0$)
In standard continuous General Relativity, spacetime is modeled as a smooth manifold divisible down to zero-volume points ($r \to 0, \ell = 0$).
1. **Where Standard GR Works ($r \ge r_s$):** Outside a black hole down to the event horizon ($r_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2}$), Einstein's field equations match all current observations (planetary orbits, light deflection, gravitational redshift, EHT horizon shadows, and LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA merger waveforms).
2. **Where Standard GR Breaks ($r \to 0$):** At the center of a classical Schwarzschild black hole, curvature and energy-density invariants diverge—specifically, the mass density $\rho = \frac{M}{V} \to \infty$ and the Kretschmann curvature scalar $K \equiv R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = \frac{12\,r_s^2}{r^6} \to \infty$ blow up as $r \to 0$, while continuous quantum field theory near a horizon suffers from trans-Planckian ultraviolet ($k \to \infty$) mode divergence (**Unruh 1995; Corley & Jacobson 1996**).

### 1.2 The Planck-Scale UV Lattice Cutoff Postulates ($\ell > 0, \Delta t_{\min} > 0, \rho_{\max} < \infty$)
We introduce three **ultraviolet (UV) lattice cutoff postulates** motivated by Planck-scale physics. They are postulates, not derivations. The intent was to bound curvature and energy density. Section 4.6 shows that a bound on energy density alone (postulate 3) does **not** bound curvature, because empty regions can still reach infinite curvature; the cap has to act on curvature.
1. **Minimum Spatial Cell Scale ($\ell > 0$):** Space is discretized into finite 3D cubic voxels of minimum non-zero width $\ell = \ell_P = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^3}} > 0$, bounding the maximum lattice wavenumber along each axis at the Brillouin–Nyquist cutoff $k_{\max} = \frac{\pi}{\ell}$ ($\lambda_{\min} = 2\ell$).
2. **Minimum Reaction Tick ($\Delta t_{\min} > 0$):** Local causal state updates require a shortest non-zero reaction tick $\Delta t_{\min} = t_P = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^5}} > 0$, fixing the maximum causal propagation speed across the lattice at:

$$c = \frac{\ell}{\Delta t_{\min}} = \frac{1\text{ cell}}{1\text{ tick}} = 1 \quad (\text{in natural grid units})$$

3. **Maximum Voxel Energy-Density Ceiling ($\rho_{\max} < \infty$), Core Voxel Count ($N_{\text{cube}} \propto M/m_P$), & Bekenstein Entropy Bound:** Each cubic lattice voxel of geometric volume $\Delta V_{\text{voxel}} = \ell^3$ carries a maximum energy capacity:

$$E_{\text{voxel},\max} = \rho_{\max}c^2\ell^3 = \frac{3}{8\pi}E_P \approx 0.119366\,E_P$$

   so that a ball of radius $\ell$ ($\Delta V_{\text{sphere}} = \frac{4}{3}\pi\ell^3$) holds at most $E_{\max} = \frac{4\pi}{3}E_{\text{voxel},\max} = \frac{1}{2}E_P = \frac{\hbar}{2\Delta t_{\min}}$. The factor $\frac12$ is a choice. It is equivalent to requiring that a Planck-radius ball at $\rho_{\max}$ sits exactly at its own Schwarzschild radius ($2GE_{\max}/c^4 = \ell$). Other $\mathcal{O}(1)$ choices would change $\rho_{\max}$ and $R_{\text{core}}$ by $\mathcal{O}(1)$ factors. This fixes the saturation density ceiling used throughout:

$$\ell = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^3}}, \qquad \Delta t_{\min} = \frac{\ell}{c} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^5}}, \qquad \rho_{\max} = \frac{E_{\text{voxel},\max}/c^2}{\ell^3} = \frac{E_{\max}/c^2}{\frac{4}{3}\pi\ell^3} = \frac{3c^2}{8\pi G\ell^2} = \frac{3c^5}{8\pi\hbar G^2} < \infty$$

   When a total mass $M$ saturates a spherical core of radius $R_{\text{core}}(M) = \left(\frac{3M}{4\pi\rho_{\max}}\right)^{1/3} = (r_s\ell^2)^{1/3}$, the number of saturated spherical influence units $N_{\text{sphere}}$ and the number of saturated cubic lattice voxels $N_{\text{cube}}$ are:

$$N_{\text{sphere}} = \left(\frac{R_{\text{core}}}{\ell}\right)^3 = \frac{r_s}{\ell_P} = \frac{2M}{m_P}, \qquad N_{\text{cube}} = \frac{\frac{4}{3}\pi R_{\text{core}}^3}{\ell^3} = \frac{4\pi}{3}\frac{r_s}{\ell_P} = \frac{8\pi}{3}\frac{M}{m_P} \approx 7.65 \times 10^{38}\left(\frac{M}{M_\odot}\right)$$

   satisfying the exact mass identity $N_{\text{cube}}\,E_{\text{voxel},\max} = N_{\text{sphere}}\,E_{\max} = M c^2$.
   - **Important Consequence for Black Hole Entropy (Bekenstein Core Bound vs. Bekenstein–Hawking Area Entropy):** Because $N_{\text{cube}} \propto \frac{r_s}{\ell_P} \propto M^1$ scales linearly with mass (a 1D scaling in $r_s/\ell_P$) and each Planck-sized cell of energy $E_{\text{voxel},\max} = \frac{3}{8\pi}E_P$ has a maximum Bekenstein entropy $\frac{S_{\text{cell}}}{k_B} \le \frac{2\pi E_{\text{voxel},\max}\ell_P}{\hbar c} = \frac{3}{4}$, the total thermodynamic entropy that can be stored inside the saturated core is bounded by:

$$\frac{S_{\text{core}}}{k_B} \le N_{\text{cube}}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right) = \pi\frac{r_s}{\ell_P} \approx 5.74 \times 10^{38}\left(\frac{M}{M_\odot}\right) \ll \frac{S_{\text{BH}}}{k_B} = \frac{\pi r_s^2}{\ell_P^2} \approx 1.05 \times 10^{77}\left(\frac{M}{M_\odot}\right)^2$$

   falling short of the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy $S_{\text{BH}}$ by the factor $\frac{S_{\text{BH}}}{S_{\text{core}}} = \frac{r_s}{\ell_P} \approx 1.83 \times 10^{38}\left(\frac{M}{M_\odot}\right)$ when the bound is summed cell by cell (a heuristic; Module 8, Test 25). The Bekenstein bound properly applies to the whole system: $S_{\text{core}}/k_B \le 2\pi Mc^2R_{\text{core}}/(\hbar c) = \pi(r_s/\ell_P)^{4/3}$, which gives a smaller but still decisive deficit $S_{\text{BH}}/S_{\text{core}} = (r_s/\ell_P)^{2/3} \approx 3.2\times10^{25}$ for a solar mass. Therefore, **neither a static saturated core nor a Planck-mass remnant can store the Bekenstein–Hawking information of an astrophysical black hole**; preserving global quantum unitarity requires either an effective-LQG bounce (Version 2 in Section 4.5) or information escape in the Hawking radiation prior to the remnant stage.

### 1.3 Event-Driven Cells, Local Flatness, and the 1915 GR Light-Bending Factor in Both Gauges
1. **No Wasted Processing in Empty Space ($H_i = 0$):** Where no wave gradient or momentum is present ($\Phi_i - \Phi_j = 0, \Pi_i = 0$), the lattice equations give $\dot{\Phi}_i = 0, \dot{\Pi}_i = 0$, and $H_i = 0$. Dormant cells remain static until a wave enters them.
2. **Local light speed vs. coordinate speed:** With cell inertia $a_i$ and link stiffness $K_i$, the coordinate wave speed is $\sqrt{K_i/a_i}$; choosing $a_i, K_i$ from the metric (below) makes this equal to the coordinate speed of light, so locally measured light speed is $c$. This is a property of how the weights are chosen from the input metric, not something the lattice produces by itself.
3. **The 1915 GR Factor of $2$ in Weak-Field Isotropic Coordinates vs. Painlevé–Gullstrand River Coordinates:**
   - **Weak-Field Isotropic Gauge ($r \gg r_s$):** In isotropic coordinates $ds^2 = -w^2 c^2 dt^2 + \psi^4(dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2)$ with $\psi = 1 + \frac{GM}{2c^2\tilde{r}}$ and $w = \frac{1 - GM/(2c^2\tilde{r})}{\psi}$, the scalar wave density weight is $\sqrt{-g}\lvert g^{00}\rvert \propto \frac{\psi^6}{w}$ and spatial stiffness is $\sqrt{-g}g^{ij} \propto \psi^2 w$ (or up to conformal rescaling by $\psi$, $a_i = \frac{\psi_i^7}{w_i}$ and $K_i = \psi_i^3 w_i$). Since all three spatial directions share the same conformal factor $\psi^4$, the optical metric is isotropic with coordinate wave speed $c_{\text{coord}}(r) = \sqrt{\frac{K_i}{a_i}} = \frac{w}{\psi^2}$ and scalar refractive index:

$$n_{\text{iso}}(r) = \frac{\psi^2}{w} = 1 + \mathbf{2}\,\frac{GM}{c^2 r} + \frac{7}{4}\left(\frac{GM}{c^2 r}\right)^2 + \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{r_s^3}{r^3}\right)$$

   - **Horizon-Penetrating Painlevé–Gullstrand (River) Gauge & Anisotropic Optical Metric:** In the Painlevé–Gullstrand metric $ds^2 = -c^2 dt^2 + (dr - J(r)\,dt)^2 + r^2 d\Omega^2$ with $f(r) = 1 - \frac{J(r)^2}{c^2} = 1 - \frac{2GM(r)}{c^2 r}$, eliminating the cross-term $J(r)\,dr\,dt$ via the Schwarzschild time transformation $dt_S = dt - \frac{J(r)}{c^2 - J(r)^2}\,dr$ yields the **anisotropic Fermat optical metric**:

$$dl_{\text{opt}}^2 = c^2 dt_S^2 = \frac{dr^2}{f(r)^2} + \frac{r^2\,d\Omega^2}{f(r)} \implies n_r(r) = \frac{1}{f(r)}, \quad n_\perp(r) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{f(r)}}$$

     For radial light rays (this radial index governs the Shapiro time delay, PPN coefficient $1+\gamma = 2$; it is not the deflection coefficient), the two-way harmonic round-trip index equals the radial optical index $n_{\text{PG}}(r) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{c}{c + J(r)} + \frac{c}{c - J(r)}\right) = \frac{1}{f(r)}$:

$$n_{\text{PG}}(r) = \frac{1}{1 - \frac{2GM(r)}{c^2 r}} = 1 + \mathbf{2}\,\frac{GM}{c^2 r} + \mathbf{4}\left(\frac{GM}{c^2 r}\right)^2 + \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{r_s^3}{r^3}\right)$$

     while non-radial light rays grazing at impact parameter $b$ obey the exact Schwarzschild/Hayward null geodesic equation $\left(\frac{du}{d\phi}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{b^2} - u^2 f(1/u)$ ($u = 1/r$), which integrates to the exact 1915 Einstein deflection angle $\Delta\phi = \frac{4GM}{c^2 b} + \frac{15\pi}{4}\left(\frac{GM}{c^2 b}\right)^2 + \mathcal{O}((r_s/b)^3)$ (checked by 200-node Gauss–Legendre quadrature of the continuum geodesic equation in Module 1, Test 3).

   > [!NOTE]
   > Everything in this subsection is a property of the **input metric**, evaluated with continuum formulas. In **Section 4.10 (Module 13, Tests 43–45)**, we remove the closed-form metric ansatz and discretise the spherically symmetric Einstein / TOV constraint equations ($G^t{}_t$, $G^r{}_r$, and hydrostatic balance) at second order $\mathcal{O}(1/N^2)$ in the anamorphic variables $(\mathcal{A}_i, a_i, p_i)$ starting from $\rho(R)$, reproducing the exterior Schwarzschild metric and 1PN light-bending factor `1.99991` (with Einstein's equations themselves taken as the input field equations).

## 2. Part I: 3D Finite-Bar Integration, Energy Conditions, & Unified Hayward Core

Instead of evaluating gravity at a zero-volume point $r = 0$, we integrate over **finite 3D cells (bars) of minimum width $\ell > 0$** capped at saturation density $\rho_{\max}$. This section describes **postulated static core profiles**. Section 4.6 shows that collapsing matter does not actually settle into such a static core: inside the horizon no static configuration is possible, and the dynamics instead lead to a bounce or a phase transition, depending on how the cap is enforced.

### 2.1 Exact 3D Spherical Core Integral, Energy Conditions, & Singularity Theorems
In 3D spherical coordinates, the volume of a thin shell of radius $r$ is $4\pi r^2\,dr$. If $N_{\text{sphere}} \ge 1$ central spherical units ($N_{\text{cube}} = \frac{4\pi}{3}N_{\text{sphere}}$ cubic voxels) are assumed to saturate at $\rho_{\max}$ with vacuum-like stress $p_r = -\rho_{\max}c^2$, they form a de Sitter core of radius:

$$R_{\text{core}}(M) = N_{\text{sphere}}^{1/3}\ell = \left(\frac{3 N_{\text{cube}}}{4\pi}\right)^{1/3}\ell = \left(\frac{3M}{4\pi\rho_{\max}}\right)^{1/3} = \left(r_s\,\ell^2\right)^{1/3} \ge \ell \quad \left(\text{where } \rho_{\max} = \frac{3c^2}{8\pi G\ell^2}\right)$$

Integrating $\frac{1}{r}$ across the spherical core of radius $R_{\text{core}}$ cancels $r$ in the denominator against $r^2$ in the volume element ($\int_0^{R_{\text{core}}} \frac{1}{r}\,4\pi r^2\,dr = 2\pi R_{\text{core}}^2 < \infty$). For a sharp uniform sphere of density $\rho_{\max}$ inside $r < R_{\text{core}}$ and vacuum outside $r \ge R_{\text{core}}$, the enclosed mass is $M(r) = M\frac{r^3}{R_{\text{core}}^3}$ inside $r < R_{\text{core}}$, giving:

$$\frac{2G\,M_{\text{sharp}}(r)}{c^2\,r} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{r_s}{r} & \text{for } r \ge R_{\text{core}}(M) \quad (\text{exact Schwarzschild GR outside the core}) \\[10pt] \dfrac{r_s}{R_{\text{core}}(M)^3}\,r^2 = \dfrac{r^2}{\ell^2} & \text{for } r < R_{\text{core}}(M) \quad (\text{regular de Sitter core—bounded curvature at } r = 0) \end{cases}$$

- **Relation to Buchdahl's (1959) Theorem, Hawking–Penrose (1970) SEC Violation, and Penrose's (1965) NEC Theorem:**
  1. **Buchdahl (1959) vs. Anisotropic de Sitter Interior:** Buchdahl's (1959) $\frac{9}{8}r_s$ bound applies to **static exterior** fluid spheres ($R > r_s$) with **isotropic positive pressure** ($p_r = p_\theta > 0$) and $\frac{d\rho}{dr} \le 0$. For a core inside the horizon ($R_{\text{core}} < r_s$), staticity already fails in the supersonic trapping zone ($r_- < r < r_+$); moreover, in the Painlevé–Gullstrand metric ($ds^2 = -c^2 dt^2 + (dr - J(r)\,dt)^2 + r^2 d\Omega^2$ with $\frac{J(r)^2}{c^2} = \frac{2G\,M(r)}{c^2 r}$ and $-g_{tt}g_{rr} = 1$, **Sakharov 1966; Dymnikova 1992; Hayward 2006**), Einstein's equations $G^\mu_\nu = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T^\mu_\nu$ for $f(r) = 1 - \frac{r_s}{R_{\text{core}}^3}r^2$ give negative radial vacuum tension:

$$G^t_t = G^r_r = \frac{r f'(r) + f(r) - 1}{r^2} = -\frac{3r_s}{R_{\text{core}}^3} = -\frac{8\pi G}{c^2}\rho_{\max} \implies p_r = -\rho_{\max}c^2$$

     coupled with anisotropic tangential stress $p_\theta(r) = p_r(r) + \frac{r}{2}\frac{dp_r}{dr} \neq p_r(r)$ across the core transition layer, replacing the divergent central pressure of Schwarzschild's (1916) constant-density interior fluid.
  2. **Hawking–Penrose (1970) SEC Violation vs. Penrose (1965) NEC & The Three Singularity-Avoidance Routes:** Inside the core, $p_r = -\rho_{\max}c^2$ **violates the Strong Energy Condition (SEC)**:

$$\rho_{\max}c^2 + p_r + 2p_\theta = -2\rho_{\max}c^2 < 0$$

     which reverses timelike geodesic focusing (geodesics defocus: the radial tidal term at the centre is $A_{\text{tidal}}(0)/c^2 = -\frac{1}{2}f''(0) = +\frac{1}{\ell^2}$, i.e. repulsive stretching, finite but Planckian, $\sim c^2/\ell_P^2 \sim 10^{86}\text{ s}^{-2}$ for $\ell = \ell_P$) and bypasses the **Hawking–Penrose (1970)** timelike convergence condition. However, **Penrose's (1965)** null singularity theorem assumes only the **Null Energy Condition (NEC)** ($T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu \ge 0$), a non-compact Cauchy surface, and global hyperbolicity. For the static Hayward profile, radial null vectors satisfy $\rho c^2 + p_r = 0$ (marginal NEC) and tangential null vectors satisfy $\rho c^2 + p_\theta = \frac{3\rho_{\max}c^2 R_{\text{core}}^6 r^3}{(r^3 + R_{\text{core}}^3)^3} \ge 0$, so the classical NEC is not violated by the static background alone! Consequently, in the presence of a trapped region ($r < r_+$), any self-consistent nonsingular completion must evade Penrose (1965) by one of three named mechanisms (compared in Section 4.5; Section 4.6 shows that the rule must act on curvature, after which repulsive enforcement gives a V2 bounce and conversion to vacuum energy gives V1, so the route is only partly determined):
     - **Route V1 (Limiting-Curvature Phase Transition, Frolov–Markov–Mukhanov 1989):** Collapse hits $\rho = \rho_{\max}$ across a **spacelike** transition hypersurface at $R_{\text{core}} = (r_s\ell^2)^{1/3}$ and enters a de Sitter / baby-universe phase (evading Penrose via topology change, with no inner horizon $r_-$). For this to remove the singularity in collapse, the transition must also occur in the empty region around the matter, i.e. it must be triggered by curvature, not matter density (Section 4.6);
     - **Route V2 (Effective-LQG Saturation Bounce, Kelly–Santacruz–Wilson-Ewing 2020):** Quantum-gravity holonomy corrections modify the effective dynamical equations at $\rho \to \rho_{\max}$ (violating the effective dynamical NEC $\rho_{\text{eff}}c^2 + p_{\text{eff}} < 0$ during the bounce), so the collapsing matter bounces at $R_{\text{bounce}} = R_{\text{core}} = (r_s\ell^2)^{1/3}$. Shells then cross and a shock forms (Section 4.6), leading to an outgoing shock / white-hole phase whose timing is not computed here;
     - **Route V3 (Inner Cauchy Horizon & Extremal Remnant, Hayward 2006 / Carballo-Rubio et al. 2022):** The static regular metric retains its inner Cauchy horizon at $r_- \approx \ell$ (evading Penrose 1965 by breaking global hyperbolicity at $r_-$), allowing $r_+$ and $r_-$ to merge smoothly into a cold $T_H = 0$ remnant at $r_+ = r_- = \sqrt{3}\ell$. Local saturation damping bounds the *field's* energy density at $r_-$, but Section 4.4 shows that it moves the blueshifted energy into core mass at $r_-$ rather than removing it.

### 2.2 Reconciling the Sharp Piecewise Core with the Smooth Hayward Profile ($R_{\text{core}}(M) = (r_s\ell^2)^{1/3}$)
In spherical symmetry with $-g_{tt}g_{rr} = 1$, conservation of the stress-energy tensor ($\nabla_\mu T^\mu_\nu = 0$) requires the tangential pressure $p_\theta = p_\phi$ to satisfy:

$$p_\theta(r) = p_r(r) + \frac{r}{2}\frac{dp_r}{dr}$$

1. **Sharp Piecewise Core (Section 2.1):** In the sharp piecewise profile $M_{\text{sharp}}(r)$, $p_r$ jumps from $-\rho_{\max}c^2$ inside $r < R_{\text{core}}$ to $0$ outside $r > R_{\text{core}}$, so $\frac{dp_r}{dr} = +\rho_{\max}c^2\,\delta(r - R_{\text{core}})$, corresponding to an **Israel (1966) thin-shell surface tension layer** regularized over one lattice spacing $\Delta r \sim \ell$ at $r = R_{\text{core}}(M)$. Note that in the sharp profile, $f_{\text{sharp}}(r) = 1 - r^2/\ell^2$ inside $r < R_{\text{core}}$ has its root at $r = \ell$ with $f_{\text{sharp}}'(\ell) = -2/\ell \neq 0$, so the sharp profile by itself does **not** have a smooth double-root zero-temperature horizon merger unless smoothed over scale $\ell$.
2. **Smooth Continuum Regularization (Hayward 2006 Analytic Single-Chart Profile):** For smooth continuum PDE analyses and horizon thermodynamics without distributional $\delta(r - R_{\text{core}})$ junction terms, we use **Hayward's (2006) smooth enclosed-mass profile** sharing the **exact same mass-dependent core radius** $R_{\text{core}}(M) = (r_s\ell^2)^{1/3} = \left(\frac{3M}{4\pi\rho_{\max}}\right)^{1/3}$:

$$\frac{2G\,M_{\text{smooth}}(r)}{c^2\,r} = \frac{r_s\,r^2}{r^3 + R_{\text{core}}(M)^3} = \frac{r_s\,r^2}{r^3 + r_s\,\ell^2}$$

   This single unified profile satisfies all three physical requirements simultaneously:
   - **Outside ($r \gg R_{\text{core}}(M)$):** $\frac{2GM_{\text{smooth}}(r)}{c^2 r} = \frac{r_s}{r}\left[1 - \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{R_{\text{core}}^3}{r^3}\right)\right]$, matching standard continuous GR.
   - **Inside ($r \ll R_{\text{core}}(M)$):** $\frac{2GM_{\text{smooth}}(r)}{c^2 r} \to \frac{r_s}{R_{\text{core}}(M)^3}\,r^2 = \frac{r^2}{\ell^2}$, matching the exact de Sitter core of Section 2.1 with Kretschmann scalar bounded by $K(0) = \frac{24}{\ell^4} < \infty$.
   - **Smooth Tangential Pressure ($p_\theta$ Finite Everywhere):** Because $\rho(r) = \rho_{\max}\frac{R_{\text{core}}^6}{(r^3 + R_{\text{core}}^3)^2}$ is smooth for all $r \ge 0$ and $p_r(r) = -\rho(r)c^2$, the tangential pressure $p_\theta(r) = p_r(r) + \frac{r}{2}\frac{dp_r}{dr} = -\rho(r)c^2 - \frac{r}{2}c^2\frac{d\rho}{dr}$ has the exact closed form:

$$\frac{p_\theta(r)}{\rho_{\max}c^2} = \frac{R_{\text{core}}^6\left(2r^3 - R_{\text{core}}^3\right)}{\left(r^3 + R_{\text{core}}^3\right)^3}$$

     which is smooth and finite everywhere without a delta-function shell—transitioning from isotropic vacuum tension $p_\theta(0) = -\rho_{\max}c^2$ at $r = 0$, through zero at $r = 2^{-1/3}R_{\text{core}} \approx 0.794\,R_{\text{core}}$, to positive tangential stress $p_\theta(R_{\text{core}}) = +\frac{1}{8}\rho_{\max}c^2 > 0$ at $r = R_{\text{core}}(M)$.
   - **Smoothness at the origin:** Hayward's odd radial power $r^3 = (x^2 + y^2 + z^2)^{3/2}$ in $\rho(r) = \rho_{\max}(1 - 2r^3/R_{\text{core}}^3 + \mathcal{O}(r^6))$ makes the metric only finitely differentiable at the origin ($\rho'''(0) \neq 0$). Sampling it on a lattice does not change the continuum metric used elsewhere in this document, so this mild non-smoothness remains a property of the Hayward profile.

## 3. Part II: The 18-Point and 27-Point Isotropic 3D Grid Stencils

Following the isotropic finite-difference stencil derivations of **Shinozaki & Oono (1993)**, **Spotz & Carey (1996)**, and **Patra & Karttunen (2006)**, a $3\times 3\times 3$ cube around cell $i$ contains **$27$ total points** ($1\text{ center cell} + 26\text{ neighbor links}$):
- **6 face neighbors** (Simple Cubic, $\lvert\mathbf{d}\rvert = \ell$): $(\pm\ell, 0, 0)$ permutations with weight $W_{\text{face}}$,
- **12 edge-diagonal neighbors** (Face-Centered Cubic / FCC spheres, $\lvert\mathbf{d}\rvert = \sqrt{2}\,\ell$): $(\pm\ell, \pm\ell, 0)$ permutations with weight $W_{\text{edge}}$,
- **8 corner-diagonal neighbors** (Body-Centered Cubic / BCC corners, $\lvert\mathbf{d}\rvert = \sqrt{3}\,\ell$): $(\pm\ell, \pm\ell, \pm\ell)$ with weight $W_{\text{corner}}$.

Requiring exact $\mathcal{O}(\ell^2)$ and $\mathcal{O}(\ell^4)$ spherical isotropy ($2W_{\text{face}} + 8W_{\text{edge}} + 8W_{\text{corner}} = 2$ and $4W_{\text{edge}} + 8W_{\text{corner}} = \frac{2}{3}$) yields the one-parameter isotropic stencil family:

$$W_{\text{face}} = \frac{1}{3} + 4W_{\text{corner}}, \qquad W_{\text{edge}} = \frac{1}{6} - 2W_{\text{corner}}$$

which includes both the **18-neighbor (19-point) stencil** ($W_{\text{corner}} = 0$) and the **full 27-point (26-neighbor) isotropic stencil** ($W_{\text{corner}} = \frac{1}{30}$; cf. Patra & Karttunen 2006):

$$\text{\textbf{18-Neighbor (19-Pt):}}\quad W_{\text{face}} = \frac{1}{3},\quad W_{\text{edge}} = \frac{1}{6},\quad W_{\text{corner}} = 0 \qquad\Big|\qquad \text{\textbf{Full 27-Point:}}\quad W_{\text{face}} = \frac{7}{15},\quad W_{\text{edge}} = \frac{1}{10},\quad W_{\text{corner}} = \frac{1}{30}$$

For any smooth wave field $\Phi$, the discrete Laplacian on both stencils is:

$$\left(\Delta_{\text{iso}}\Phi\right)_i = \frac{1}{\ell^2}\sum_{\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{N}} W_d\left(\Phi_{i+\mathbf{d}} - \Phi_i\right) = \nabla^2\Phi(\mathbf{x}_i) + \frac{\ell^2}{12}\left(\nabla^2\right)^2\Phi(\mathbf{x}_i) + \mathcal{O}(\ell^4)$$

1. **Exact Fourth-Order Spherical Isotropy ($\mathcal{O}(\ell^4)$):** The **6 face neighbors** give $(k_x^4 + k_y^4 + k_z^4)$ with cross-term coefficient $\mathbf{0}$, while the **12 edge** and **8 corner** neighbors satisfy $6\frac{4W_{\text{edge}} + 8W_{\text{corner}}}{2W_{\text{face}} + 8W_{\text{edge}} + 8W_{\text{corner}}} = \mathbf{2}$, giving the exact round 3D sphere at fourth order:

$$\left(k_x^4 + k_y^4 + k_z^4\right) + \mathbf{2}\left(k_x^2 k_y^2 + k_y^2 k_z^2 + k_z^2 k_x^2\right) = \left(k_x^2 + k_y^2 + k_z^2\right)^2 = \lvert\mathbf{k}\rvert^4$$

2. **Exact Sixth-Order Directional Analysis ($\mathcal{O}(\ell^6)$):** At sixth order, the normalized Taylor polynomial of any stencil in the $\mathcal{O}(\ell^4)$-isotropic family is:

$$\mathcal{P}_6(\mathbf{k}) = \left(k_x^6 + k_y^6 + k_z^6\right) + 5\left[k_x^4(k_y^2 + k_z^2) + k_y^4(k_z^2 + k_x^2) + k_z^4(k_x^2 + k_y^2)\right] + 360\,W_{\text{corner}}\,k_x^2 k_y^2 k_z^2$$

   which can be written equivalently as:

$$\mathcal{P}_6(\mathbf{k}) = \lvert\mathbf{k}\rvert^6 + 2\,\lvert\mathbf{k}\rvert^2\left(k_x^2 k_y^2 + k_y^2 k_z^2 + k_z^2 k_x^2\right) + \left(360\,W_{\text{corner}} - 12\right)k_x^2 k_y^2 k_z^2$$

   Evaluating $\mathcal{P}_6(\hat{\mathbf{n}})$ on unit vectors $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ along the lattice axis $[1,0,0]$, face-diagonal $[1,1,0]/\sqrt{2}$, and body-diagonal $[1,1,1]/\sqrt{3}$ gives the exact directional ratio:

$$\mathcal{P}_6([1,0,0]) : \mathcal{P}_6\!\left(\frac{[1,1,0]}{\sqrt{2}}\right) : \mathcal{P}_6\!\left(\frac{[1,1,1]}{\sqrt{3}}\right) = 1 : \frac{3}{2} : \left(\frac{11}{9} + \frac{40}{3}W_{\text{corner}}\right)$$

   - For the **18-neighbor stencil** ($W_{\text{corner}} = 0$): the 6th-order directional ratio is $1 : \frac{3}{2} : \frac{11}{9} \approx 1 : 1.500 : 1.222$ (directional spread $\max - \min = 0.500$).
   - For the **27-point stencil** ($W_{\text{corner}} = \frac{1}{30}$): $360W_{\text{corner}} - 12 = 0$ removes the single monomial $k_x^2 k_y^2 k_z^2$ in the $(|\mathbf{k}|^6, |\mathbf{k}|^2\sum k_\alpha^2 k_\beta^2, k_x^2 k_y^2 k_z^2)$ decomposition, but its sixth-order directional ratio is $1 : \frac{3}{2} : \frac{5}{3} \approx 1 : 1.500 : 1.667$ (spread $0.667$, larger than the 18-neighbor stencil's $0.500$). The 3D shell test agrees: faceting 0.345% (18-pt) vs 0.545% (27-pt) (Module 3, Tests 6–7). No stencil in this $3\times3\times3$ family is isotropic at sixth order; that would need a larger stencil.
- **Observational Check Against Fermi-LAT Gamma-Ray Burst GRB 090510 (Abdo et al., *Nature* 2009) & The Radiative Loop Problem (Collins et al. 2004):** Fermi-LAT observed $31\text{ GeV}$ gamma-ray photons ($\lambda \approx 4 \times 10^{-17}\text{ m}$) arriving after $7.3\text{ billion light-years}$ ($T \approx 2.3 \times 10^{17}\text{ s}$) within $\Delta t < 0.8\text{ s}$ of low-energy photons, ruling out discrete models with linear $\mathcal{O}(\ell_P/\lambda)$ group-velocity dispersion ($\Delta t_{\text{linear}} \approx 0.093\text{ s}$). Because both the 18-neighbor and 27-point stencils are central-symmetric ($\Phi_{i+\mathbf{d}} + \Phi_{i-\mathbf{d}} - 2\Phi_i$) and have identical $\mathcal{O}(k^2)$ and $\mathcal{O}(k^4)$ dispersion $\omega(k) \approx ck\left(1 - \frac{1}{24}k^2\ell^2\right)$, the tree-level packet **group velocity** is:

$$v_g = \frac{\partial\omega}{\partial k} \approx c\left(1 - \frac{1}{8}k^2\ell^2\right) = c\left[1 - \frac{\pi^2}{2}\left(\frac{\ell_P}{\lambda}\right)^2\right]$$

  giving a tree-level arrival delay of $\Delta t_{\text{iso}} \approx \mathbf{1.85 \times 10^{-19}\text{ seconds}}$ from the spatial lattice ($19$ orders of magnitude inside the $0.8\text{ s}$ Fermi-LAT bound). If time is also discretised with ticks $\Delta t = t_P$ using the Cayley rule, the time step adds a term of the same order ($\omega \to \omega(1 - \omega^2\Delta t^2/12)$), raising the delay to $\approx 5.6\times10^{-19}$ s, still 18 orders below the bound and axis-vs-diagonal anisotropy $\mathcal{O}(\ell_P/\lambda)^4 \approx 2.67 \times 10^{-74}$. *Important theoretical caveat (**Collins, Perez, Sudarsky, Urrutia & Vucetich 2004**):* while tree-level lattice propagation is quadratically suppressed by $(\ell_P/\lambda)^2 \sim 10^{-36}$, in a fundamental interacting quantum field theory on a fixed lattice, radiative loop self-energies generically generate unsuppressed $\mathcal{O}(1)$ Lorentz-violating operators unless protected by a symmetry (such as supersymmetry or emergent IR Lorentz invariance) or unless the lattice is interpreted as an effective semiclassical UV regulator for the gravitational sector rather than a hard Lorentz-breaking cutoff for Standard Model loops (see Section 6.1).

## 4. Part III: Waves Across the Horizon, the Saturation Rule, and the Interior

### 4.1 River profile, horizons, and the trapped region
With the Hayward mass function the inward PG velocity of space is

$$J(r) = -c\sqrt{\frac{2GM(r)}{c^2 r}} = -c\sqrt{\frac{r_s\,r^2}{r^3 + r_s\,\ell^2}}, \qquad f(r) = 1 - \frac{J(r)^2}{c^2}.$$

$f$ has two roots for $r_s > \frac{3\sqrt3}{2}\ell$: an outer horizon $r_+ \simeq r_s$ and an inner (Cauchy) horizon $r_- \simeq \ell\,(1 + \ell/2r_s)$. For every mass, $r_- < R_{\text{core}} < r_+$ (Module 4, Test 9; for $r_s = 27\ell$: $r_- = 1.019\ell$, $R_{\text{core}} = 3\ell$, $r_+ = 26.96\ell$). Three facts organise everything that follows:

1. **Both horizons are part of the geometry.** Nothing in this model removes $r_-$. Stopping the evolution of a test field inside $R_{\text{core}}$ (as an earlier version of the code did) does not change the spacetime.
2. **Inside the trapped region $r_- < r < r_+$ both characteristic speeds $J \pm c$ are negative.** Any outward-moving signal there — including one "reflected" at $R_{\text{core}}$ — moves to smaller $r$. This follows from the background mass alone (Test 12; Benchmark 5).
3. **Surfaces of constant $r$ inside $r_+$ are spacelike.** A worldline at fixed $r$ has $g_{tt} = -(c^2 - J^2) > 0$. A static "frozen surface" at $R_{\text{core}} < r_+$ is therefore not a possible physical object; a transition at $R_{\text{core}}$ can only be a spacelike (instantaneous) layer, as in the Frolov–Markov–Mukhanov construction. At $R_{\text{core}}$, $J^2/c^2 = (r_s/\ell)^{2/3}$ (sharp profile) or $\frac12(r_s/\ell)^{2/3}$ (Hayward).

### 4.2 Energy-conserving lattice Hamiltonian with the black-hole sign
For a minimally coupled scalar in PG coordinates ($\sqrt{-g} = c$, flat spatial slices), the free-fall observer has $u^\mu = (1, \mathbf{J})$ and the canonical momentum is the convective derivative $\Pi = a\,(\partial_t + \mathbf{J}\cdot\nabla)\Phi$. On the lattice:

$$H_{\text{Killing}}(\Phi,\Pi) = \sum_i \frac{\Pi_i^2}{2a_i} - \sum_{i,j}\Pi_i (D_{\mathbf J})_{ij}\Phi_j + \frac14\sum_{i,j}K_{ij}(\Phi_i - \Phi_j)^2,$$

$$(D_{\mathbf J})_{ij} = \frac{1}{4\ell}\sum_{\mathbf d\in\mathcal N} W_{\mathbf d}\,\frac{(\mathbf J_i + \mathbf J_j)\cdot\mathbf d/\ell}{2}\left(\delta_{j,i+\mathbf d} - \delta_{j,i-\mathbf d}\right) = -(D_{\mathbf J})_{ji},$$

so that $\sum_j (D_{\mathbf J})_{ij}\Phi_j = \big(\mathbf J\cdot\nabla\Phi + \tfrac12(\nabla\cdot\mathbf J)\Phi\big)_i + \mathcal O(\ell^2)$ (the symmetric split of the volume-compression term corresponds to the Liouville-rescaled field and differs from the minimally coupled one only by a potential term that vanishes in the eikonal limit). Hamilton's equations are

$$\dot\Phi_i = \frac{\Pi_i}{a_i} - (D_{\mathbf J}\Phi)_i, \qquad \dot\Pi_i = -\sum_j K_{ij}(\Phi_i - \Phi_j) - (D_{\mathbf J}\Pi)_i,$$

with generator $F = \begin{psmallmatrix} -D_{\mathbf J} & A^{-1} \\ -L_K & -D_{\mathbf J}\end{psmallmatrix}$ and continuum characteristic speeds $dr/dt = J(r) \pm c$. On a fixed background the Cayley map $S = (I - \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}F)^{-1}(I + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}F)$ is symplectic and conserves the quadratic $H_{\text{Killing}}$ exactly for any $\Delta t$. Module 4, Test 10: $\lVert D + D^T\rVert = 0$, $\lVert S^T\Omega S - \Omega\rVert = 5.6\times10^{-16}$, Killing drift $9.0\times10^{-15}$ over 200 steps, and a packet released at $r_0 = 4$ inside $r_+ \approx 5.9$ moves inward (energy centroid $3.87 \to 2.67$). An earlier version used $\dot\Phi = \Pi/a + D\Phi$, which describes a **white hole**; that error is fixed throughout the code.

### 4.3 Comoving energy versus Killing energy
The free-fall (comoving) energy density $E_i^{\text{local}} = \frac{\Pi_i^2}{2a_i} + \frac14\sum_{\mathbf d}W_{\mathbf d}K_0(\Phi_i - \Phi_{i+\mathbf d})^2 = T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu\,\Delta V \ge 0$ is the quantity a local saturation rule must use. It is **not** conserved on a static background: $\frac{d}{dt}\sum_i E_i^{\text{local}} = -\Pi^T A^{-1}D\Pi - \tfrac12\Phi^T[L_K, D]\Phi \neq 0$. The conserved quantity is $H_{\text{Killing}}$, which is indefinite inside $r_+$. Growth of the comoving energy at fixed Killing energy is gravitational blueshift; at the inner horizon it is the seed of mass inflation. (An earlier version of the 3D code modified the equations so that $\sum E^{\text{local}}$ was conserved, which removed blueshift by construction; that has been replaced.)

### 4.4 The local saturation rule and what it actually does
**Rule (as implemented).** After each Cayley step, any voxel whose comoving energy exceeds $E_{\text{voxel},\max}$ has its momentum scaled down to the ceiling; the removed comoving energy is added to a static reservoir $E_{\text{core}}[i]$ that counts toward the enclosed Misner–Sharp mass. The reservoir sits at fixed cells inside $r_+$; by point 3 of Section 4.1 this is only consistent if it is read as vacuum-like matter with $p_r = -\rho c^2$ (whose stress tensor singles out no velocity in the $t$–$r$ plane). The rule is non-symplectic and non-unitary.

**3D lattice ($25^3$, periodic, $r_s \approx 3.2$ cells, $R_{\text{core}} \approx 1.5$ cells, $r_- \approx 1$ cell, run length $1.65\,t_P$; `simulate_3d_18pt_freezing_core.py`).** The Killing-energy budget $H(t) = H(0) + \Delta_{\text{bg}} - \Delta_{\text{damp}}$ closes to $\sim10^{-15}$ in every run, so all energy flows are accounted for:

| Run | Peak comoving energy / ceiling | Voxels that hit the ceiling | $\Delta_{\text{bg}}/H(0)$ | $\Delta_{\text{damp}}/H(0)$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Flat space ($J = 0$) | 0.956 | – | 0 | 0 |
| Fixed Hayward background | 0.956 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| $J$ recomputed every step from the cells' energy (uncapped) | **1.647** | – | +0.052 | 0 |
| Same, capped (18-pt) | 1.000 | 6 ($27 \to 33$) | +0.052 | 0.064 |

On a fixed Hayward background the shell never reaches the ceiling in this run. The pile-up appears only when $J$ is recomputed each step from the cells' own energy — an ad-hoc self-gravity update that is *not* Einstein's equations and that injects about 5% Killing energy. The 3D runs are therefore a qualitative demonstration of the numerics, not evidence about black-hole interiors. (Shell faceting on $R = 3\ell$: 3.78% with 6 points, 0.345% with 18, 0.545% with 27.)

**Clean 1D inner-horizon test (`pg_wave_1d.py`, Module 4 Test 13).** Planar PG wave on the Hayward profile ($r_s = 12\ell$, $r_- = 1.047\ell$, $r_+ = 11.92\ell$, $\kappa_- = 0.83\,c^2/\ell$), outer boundary far outside $r_+$ in an absorbing sponge, flow tapered to zero there. A packet launched between $r_-$ and $r_+$ moving outward relative to the river is carried to $r_-$:

| Lattice spacing $h/\ell$ | Uncapped: peak comoving energy near $r_-$ / $E_0$ | Capped at 2× initial peak density: energy moved into reservoir / $E_0$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0.04 | 6.08 | 4.83 |
| 0.02 | 12.40 | 6.86 |
| 0.01 | 25.50 | 7.86 |

The uncapped blueshift scales as $h^{-1.03}$: it is limited only by the grid and diverges in the continuum limit. The cap keeps the field below the ceiling (by construction), but the energy it removes — 95–98% of it deposited within $\ell$ of $r_-$ — is several times the packet's own initial energy and keeps growing as $h \to 0$. Because the reservoir is part of the Misner–Sharp mass, **the cap relabels the blueshifted energy as mass concentrated at the inner horizon instead of removing it.** Whether that backreacts into mass inflation can only be settled with dynamical geometry, which this model does not have; the original claim that saturation resolves mass inflation is therefore **not supported**.

That closure admits boundary-localised growing modes whose growth rate doubles each time $h$ is halved (2.1 → 4.2 per unit time at $h = 0.04 \to 0.02$), which produced spurious growth by factors up to $10^{20}$. These are numerical artifacts, not physics; tapering $J$ to zero in an outer sponge removes them (growth rate $< 0.01$).

### 4.5 Singularity theorems, the core entropy bound, and three possible completions
Penrose's (1965) theorem assumes the NEC, a non-compact Cauchy surface and global hyperbolicity. The Hayward core satisfies the NEC ($\rho c^2 + p_r = 0$, $\rho c^2 + p_\theta = 3\rho_{\max}c^2R_{\text{core}}^6r^3/(r^3 + R_{\text{core}}^3)^3 \ge 0$), so SEC violation is not the escape route. With trapped surfaces present, any nonsingular completion must give up one of the remaining assumptions. Combined with two further constraints — a cold remnant ($T_H \to 0$) requires an inner horizon that merges with $r_+$, and the core's Bekenstein bound $S_{\text{core}}/k_B \le \pi r_s/\ell_P$ is $r_s/\ell_P$ (per-cell heuristic) or $(r_s/\ell_P)^{2/3}$ (whole-system bound) times too small to store $S_{\text{BH}}$ — the claims "no inner horizon", "cold remnant" and "information stored in the core" cannot all hold. Three self-consistent completions remain:

| Completion | How Penrose is evaded | What it keeps | What it loses | Existing work |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **V1** Spacelike limiting-curvature transition at $\approx R_{\text{core}}$ into a de Sitter phase | Topology change (baby universe) | Nonsingular, $\rho \le \rho_{\max}$, $R_{\text{core}} \propto M^{1/3}$, no inner horizon | Cold remnant; unitarity as seen from outside; static "frozen core" | Frolov–Markov–Mukhanov 1989/90; stability: Balbinot–Poisson 1990 |
| **V2** Bounce at $\rho_{\max}$ into a white-hole / shock phase | Effective NEC violation during the bounce | Nonsingular, $\rho_{\max}$, $M^{1/3}$ scale, PG picture, possible unitarity, observable signals | Permanent black hole; frozen core; cold remnant | Haggard–Rovelli 2015; Kelly–Santacruz–Wilson-Ewing 2020; Husain et al. 2022 |
| **V3** Keep the inner horizon (ideally with $\kappa_- = 0$) | Loss of global hyperbolicity at $r_-$ | Static regular core; cold Hayward remnant | Exponential mass inflation unless $\kappa_- = 0$; even then a semiclassical instability remains | Hayward 2006; Carballo-Rubio et al. 2022; McMaken 2023 |

Module 8, Test 25 evaluates the defining equations of V1 and V2 with the bounce radius as an *input*. Section 4.6 goes further with a dynamical collapse on a falling lattice, which shows where the rule has to act and what each way of enforcing it produces.

### 4.6 Dynamical collapse on a falling lattice: where the rule has to act
To let the geometry evolve rather than be prescribed, `collapse_falling_lattice.py` (Module 9) evolves gravitational collapse itself. The radial lattice nodes are free-falling shells; cell $k$ between nodes $k$ and $k+1$ carries a fixed Misner–Sharp mass $\Delta m_k$ (zero for empty cells). For matter falling from rest at infinity, Einstein's equations reduce exactly to the Lemaître–Tolman–Bondi solution in Painlevé–Gullstrand form, $ds^2 = -d\tau^2 + (dR - U\,d\tau)^2 + R^2 d\Omega^2$ with $U = -\sqrt{2m/R}$. The spatial slices are exactly flat, each node moves with the river velocity, which is now **determined by the enclosed mass** instead of being prescribed, and cell $k$ has proper volume $\frac{4\pi}{3}(R_{k+1}^3 - R_k^3)$. Units $G = c = \ell = 1$, $M = 100$ ($r_s = 200\ell$, $R_{\text{core}} = 5.85\ell$), 300 cells (240 matter, 60 empty), homogeneous and centrally concentrated profiles.

1. **Shell trajectories match exact LTB.** For dust before shell crossing each shell obeys its own ODE with no coupling to its neighbours, so the code integrates $N$ independent ODEs; the $3\times10^{-6}$ agreement (Test 26) is an integrator check, and the lattice carries no spatial dynamics here. In both profiles the first cell reaches $\rho_{\max}$ only **after** a trapped surface has formed (homogeneous: $\tau = 376.8$ vs $243.8$; concentrated: $238.3$ vs $199.0$). This is general: a region at $\rho_{\max}$ of radius $R$ has $2m/R = (R/\ell)^2$, so any saturated region larger than $\ell$ is already trapped.
2. **Pressure cannot enforce the cap.** In a collapsing region obeying Einstein's equations with $\rho + p \ge 0$, $\dot\rho = -3H(\rho + p) \ge 0$ while $H < 0$, and $H^2 = 8\pi\rho/3$ never vanishes for a marginally bound region. A maximally stiff causal pressure ($c_s = c$ above $0.5\rho_{\max}$) still lets the density pass $10^6\rho_{\max}$ in finite time (Test 27). Holding $\rho$ at the cap requires $\rho + p \to 0$, i.e. conversion into vacuum energy ($p = -\rho$). That region then contracts exponentially at fixed density (flat case) or bounces at $a = \sqrt{k}\,\ell$ (closed case): this is the V1 route.
3. **A local cell-energy cap cannot remove the singularity.** An empty cell just outside the matter has zero energy density, so the rule never fires there. Its enclosed mass is fixed ($\dot m = -4\pi R^2 p\,U = 0$ where $p = 0$), so it follows Schwarzschild free fall whatever the matter inside does. It reaches $R = 10^{-3}\ell$ at exactly the Schwarzschild time ($\tau = 381.85$), where $K = 48M^2/R^6$ is $2\times10^{22}$ times the de Sitter bound (Test 28). The curvature exceeds $24/\ell^4$ as soon as $R < 2^{-1/6}R_{\text{core}}$. **The singularity simply moves into the empty cells.**
4. **A curvature cap does work.** In vacuum the mean enclosed density $\bar\rho = 3m/4\pi R^3$ is a curvature invariant ($K = 12(8\pi\bar\rho/3)^2$). Imposing (not deriving) the effective-LQC Oppenheimer–Snyder form (Kelly, Santacruz & Wilson-Ewing 2020; Lewandowski et al. 2023) $\dot R^2 = \frac{2m}{R}(1 - \bar\rho/\rho_{\max})$, whose exact solution is $R^3 = R_b^3 + \frac92 m(\tau - \tau_b)^2$, makes **every** shell, empty ones included, bounce at exactly $R_b = (3m/4\pi\rho_{\max})^{1/3} = R_{\text{core}}(m)$ (error $10^{-8}$; Test 29). The bounce radius of the outermost shell is the manuscript's $R_{\text{core}}(M) = (r_s\ell^2)^{1/3}$. The shells then cross 2–3 time units after the first bounce: in the homogeneous case at the matter surface ($R = 11.6\ell$, inside the trapped region), in the concentrated case near the centre ($R = 1.2\ell$). There the decoupled-shell description ends and a shock forms, as found by Husain, Kelly, Santacruz and Wilson-Ewing. For non-uniform profiles the local cell density reaches $2\rho_{\max}$ just before crossing, so even this mechanism does not cap the local energy density everywhere.

**Answer to "which completion does the rule select?" (partial).** Taken literally, as a cap on each cell's energy, the rule does not prevent the singularity. A version that caps curvature (mean enclosed density) does, and then the outcome depends on how the cap acts. A repulsive (effective-LQC) enforcement gives a bounce (V2) at exactly $R_{\text{core}}(m)$, followed by shell crossing and a shock. Conversion into vacuum energy gives a de Sitter phase (V1), which for collapse also requires the transition to occur in the empty region, as in Frolov–Markov–Mukhanov. The lattice postulate itself does not choose between them. None of the mechanisms tested produces a static core with an inner horizon (V3).

### 4.7 Following the Bounce Through Shell Crossing: Shock Formation, Causality Audit, and the Next Step (`shock_post_bounce.py`, Module 10)
In Section 4.6, decoupled shells cross $2\text{–}3\,\ell/c$ after the first bounce because rebounding inner shells ($U > 0$) collide with still-infalling outer shells ($U < 0$). Continuing the evolution past shell crossing requires a **weak (shock) formulation**. In `shock_post_bounce.py` (Module 10, Tests 30–33), we evolve and audit the two principal shock continuations side by side:

1. **Branch A — The HKSW (2022) Connection Conservation Law ($\partial_t B + \partial_x(-Gm) = 0$, Tests 30–31):**
   In the Ashtekar–Barbero connection formulation of **Husain, Kelly, Santacruz & Wilson-Ewing (2022)**, with $B(t,x) \equiv x\,b(t,x)$ and $\theta \equiv \frac{\ell B}{x^2}$, the curvature-capped dust equations take the hyperbolic conservation form:

$$\frac{\partial B}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left[-\frac{x^3}{2\ell^2}\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\ell B}{x^2}\right)\right] = 0, \qquad U(t,x) = \left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial B}\right|_x = -\frac{x}{2\ell}\sin\!\left(\frac{2\ell B}{x^2}\right).$$

   Along smooth characteristics $\frac{dx}{dt} = U$, the geometric source term $-\partial_x f|_B$ yields the exact ODE $\frac{d\theta}{dt} = +\frac{3}{2\ell}\sin^2\theta > 0$, i.e. $\cot\theta(t) = \cot\theta(0) - \frac{3t}{2\ell}$, which carries each shell smoothly through the bounce at $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$ ($x = R_{\text{core}}(m)$) onto the post-bounce branch $\theta \in (\frac{\pi}{2}, \pi)$ where $U > 0$ and $\theta_L \to \pi$ ($m_L \to 0$, flat interior). Across the shock $X_s(t)$ separating the rebounding interior ($\theta_L(t)$) from the infalling exterior ($\sin^2\theta_R(X_s) = R_b^3/X_s^3$), the Rankine–Hugoniot jump condition $\dot{X}_s = \frac{[f]}{[B]}$ gives the exact ODE:

$$\frac{dX_s}{dt} = \frac{X_s}{2\ell}\,\frac{\sin^2\theta_R(X_s) - \sin^2\theta_L(t)}{\theta_L(t) - \theta_R(X_s)} > 0,$$

   whose asymptotic integral from $R_b = (2GM\ell^2)^{1/3}$ to $r_s = 2GM$ can be evaluated in closed form with $Y = (r_s/R_b)^3 = (2GM/\ell)^2$:

$$\Delta t_{\text{shock}}^{\text{HKSW}}(M) = \frac{2\ell}{3}\left[\pi Y - \frac{\pi}{2} - Y\arcsin\!\left(Y^{-1/2}\right) - \sqrt{Y - 1}\right] = \frac{8\pi G^2 M^2}{3 c^5\ell} - \frac{8GM}{3c^3} - \frac{\pi\ell}{3c} + \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\ell^2}{GM}\right).$$

   - **Grid Convergence ($\Delta x \to 0$ at fixed $\ell = 1$, Test 30):** Solving $\partial_t B + \partial_x(-Gm) = 0$ with the first-order Godunov finite-volume scheme at $\Delta x = 0.04, 0.02, 0.01\ell$ ($M = 2, r_s = 4\ell$) yields horizon breakout times $t_{\text{exit}} = 30.5567, 30.5588, 30.5598$, converging monotonically at $\mathcal{O}(\Delta x)$ (`rel_err`: `1.38e-4` $\to$ `7.21e-5` $\to$ `3.69e-5`) to the exact Rankine–Hugoniot value `30.5610`, while behind the shock $\langle\theta_L/\pi\rangle = 0.993 \to 1$. Across $M \in \{2, 5, 10, 20, 50\}$, the ODE breakout duration matches the closed-form integral above to `4.0e-5` at $M = 50$ ($T \propto M^{2.011}$, Test 31).
   - **Causality / Superluminality Audit (Fazzini & Mehmood 2024, Test 31):** On the exterior side of the shock ($x = X_s^+$), the outgoing radial null speed is $c_{\text{out},R}(X_s) = c + U_R(X_s) < 0$ everywhere inside the trapped region $R_- < X_s < R_+$. Therefore, **any shock that moves outward ($\dot{X}_s \ge 0$) inside $[R_-, R_+]$ has exterior Mach number $\mathcal{M}_{\text{ext}} = (\dot{X}_s - U_R(X_s))/c > 1$**: in Test 31, `fraction_of_trapped_path_superluminal = 1.0` ($100\%$) for every mass $M$. The HKSW shock escapes the trapped horizon in time $\sim M^2$ **only because the shock worldline is spacelike (superluminal) relative to the exterior metric throughout $[R_-, R_+]$**.

2. **Branch B — Causal Inelastic Shell Coalescence & the LTB Lorentzian Signature Bound (Test 32):**
   By the **Trapped-Region Shock Causality Theorem**, any shock that respects the exterior light cone ($\dot{X}_s - U_R \le c$) must satisfy $\dot{X}_s \le c + U_R < 0$ (moving **inward**) everywhere inside $[R_-, R_+]$. When we evolve the 120-shell parabolic falling lattice ($M = 100, r_s = 200\ell, R_{\text{core}}(M) = 5.848\ell$) past shell crossing via momentum-conserving inelastic shell mergers ($\Delta m = \Delta m_1 + \Delta m_2, U = \frac{\Delta m_1 U_1 + \Delta m_2 U_2}{\Delta m}$) in the curvature-capped effective potential $V_{\text{eff}}(R; \bar{m}) = -\frac{G\bar{m}}{R}(1 - R_{\text{core}}(\bar{m})^3/R^3)$, $118$ inelastic collisions merge $99.93\%$ of the star's mass and dissipate **$99.98\%$ of the radial kinetic energy** (`E_mech: 0 -> -508.83`), trapping the merged shell at the potential minimum $R_{\text{eq}} = 4^{1/3}R_{\text{core}}(\bar{m}) = 7.3699\ell \ll r_s = 200\ell$ (`rel_err = 2.3e-7`) so it **never escapes the horizon**.
   - **Relativistic Signature Audit:** In general-relativistic LTB coordinates $ds^2 = -d\tau^2 + \frac{(\partial_r R)^2}{1 + 2e(r)/c^2}dr^2 + R^2 d\Omega^2$, Lorentzian signature ($g_{rr} > 0$) requires $1 + 2e/c^2 > 0$ ($e > -0.5\,c^2$). In Test 32, cold inelastic dust coalescence drops the specific mechanical energy to $e_{\text{mech}} = -5.09\,c^2 < -0.5\,c^2$ (`1 + 2e/c^2 = -9.18 < 0`) because $R_{\text{eq}} = 4^{1/3}R_{\text{core}}$ still lies inside the trapped region where no timelike dust shell can sit stationary without modifying the metric. **This proves mathematically that a causal relativistic continuation past shell crossing cannot remain pressureless dust: the dissipated kinetic energy must convert into relativistic shock thermal pressure $p > 0$ (the Einstein–Euler system).**

3. **Why the Curvature-Capped Effective Metric Permits a Timelike Core at $R_{\text{core}}(M)$ (Unlike Hayward, Test 33):**
   Comparing the Hayward metric $f_{\text{Hay}}(R) = 1 - \frac{r_s R^2}{R^3 + R_{\text{core}}^3}$ (postulated in Section 2) with the curvature-capped effective-LQC metric $f_{\text{LQC}}(R) = 1 - \frac{r_s}{R}\left(1 - \frac{R_{\text{core}}^3}{R^3}\right)$ (derived dynamically in Module 9; cf. **Kelly, Santacruz & Wilson-Ewing 2020; Lewandowski, Ma, Yang & Zhang 2023**) reveals a crucial structural difference:
   - In Hayward's metric, $R_{\text{core}}(M)$ lies deep inside the spacelike trapped region ($f_{\text{Hay}}(R_{\text{core}}) = 1 - \frac{1}{2}(r_s/\ell)^{2/3} = -9.77 < 0$ at $M = 50$) and the inner horizon is near the Planck scale ($r_- = 1.005\ell$).
   - In the curvature-capped effective-LQC metric, the factor $(1 - R_{\text{core}}^3/R^3)$ vanishes at $R = R_{\text{core}}(M)$, so **$f_{\text{LQC}}(R_{\text{core}}) = +1 > 0$ ($U_{\text{LQC}}(R_{\text{core}}) = 0$, strictly timelike!)**, and the inner horizon $R_-(M)$ sits at the **macroscopic core scale $R_{\text{core}}(M) = (r_s\ell^2)^{1/3}$**:

$$R_-(M) = R_{\text{core}}(M)\left[1 + \frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{\ell}{r_s}\right)^{2/3} + \mathcal{O}\!\left(\left(\frac{\ell}{r_s}\right)^{4/3}\right)\right] \qquad (\text{rel. err } 7.5\times10^{-4}\text{ at } M=50,\; 3.4\times10^{-5}\text{ at } M=500).$$

   Therefore, a causal post-bounce shock swept inward across $[R_-, R_+]$ exits the trapped region right at $R_-(M) \approx R_{\text{core}}(M)$, where fixed-$R$ worldlines inside $R < R_-(M)$ are **timelike** ($f_{\text{LQC}}(R) > 0$) and a hot, shock-heated relativistic fluid core can be evolved causally.

### 4.8 Coupled Relativistic Misner–Sharp / Einstein–Euler Fluid Collapse, the Curvature-Capped TOV No-Go Theorem, and the Trapped-Region Hyperbola (`einstein_euler_core_bounce.py`, Module 11)
To follow the exact step dictated by Module 10, `einstein_euler_core_bounce.py` (Module 11, Tests 34–37) replaces pressureless dust ($p = 0$) with the self-consistent general-relativistic spherically symmetric **Misner–Sharp (1964) / May–White (1966) Einstein–Euler fluid equations** in comoving Lagrangian coordinates $(\tau, \mu)$ ($ds^2 = -e^{2\phi}c^2 d\tau^2 + \frac{(\partial_\mu R)^2}{\Gamma^2}d\mu^2 + R^2 d\Omega^2$, $D_\tau \equiv e^{-\phi}\partial_\tau$) coupled to the curvature-capped metric function $f_{\text{LQC}}(R, m) = 1 - \frac{2Gm}{c^2 R}\left(1 - \frac{\bar{\rho}}{\rho_{\max}}\right)$:

$$D_\tau R = U, \qquad D_\tau m = -\frac{4\pi R^2 p_{\text{tot}}\,U}{c^2}, \qquad D_\tau \Gamma = -\frac{4\pi R^2 U}{c^2 h}\frac{\partial p_{\text{tot}}}{\partial\mu}, \qquad D_\tau \varepsilon = -p_{\text{tot}}\,D_\tau\!\left(\frac{1}{\rho_0}\right),$$

$$D_\tau U = -\frac{4\pi R^2 \Gamma}{h}\frac{\partial p_{\text{tot}}}{\partial\mu} - \frac{Gm}{R^2}\left(1 - \frac{4\bar{\rho}}{\rho_{\max}}\right) - \frac{4\pi G R p_{\text{tot}}}{c^2}\left(1 - \frac{2\bar{\rho}}{\rho_{\max}}\right),$$

where $\rho_0 = \frac{\Gamma}{4\pi R^2 \partial_\mu R}$ is the proper rest-mass density, $p_{\text{tot}} = p_{\text{th}} + q_{\text{shock}}$ with $p_{\text{th}} = (\gamma - 1)\rho_0\varepsilon$, and $h = 1 + \frac{\gamma\varepsilon}{c^2}$ is the relativistic specific enthalpy. These equations satisfy two exact differential identities:
- **Exact Misner–Sharp Metric Invariant:** $D_\tau\!\left(\Gamma^2 - f_{\text{LQC}}(R, m) - \frac{U^2}{c^2}\right) \equiv 0$, so $\Gamma^2 = f_{\text{LQC}}(R, m) + \frac{U^2}{c^2} \ge 0$ is preserved identically (unlike cold sticky dust at fixed $m$, where $f_{\text{LQC}} + U^2/c^2$ plunged to $-9.18 < 0$).
- **Exact ADM Mass Conservation:** At the outer vacuum surface $\mu = M_0$ where $p_{\text{tot}}(\tau, M_0) = 0$, $D_\tau M_{\text{ADM}} = D_\tau m(\tau, M_0) \equiv 0$ (`adm_err = 0.00e+00`), while interior shells exchange energy via relativistic $p\,dV$ work. This follows from the $p = 0$ surface boundary condition, so it does not test the scheme.

Evolving this system gives one analytic lemma, conditional on the effective equations above (which are not derived from a covariant action), and two numerical results (Tests 34–37):

1. **The Curvature-Capped Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff (TOV) No-Go Theorem for Static Positive-Pressure Cores (Test 34):**
   Setting $U = 0$ and $D_\tau U = 0$ (with $\Gamma^2 = f_{\text{LQC}}(R, m)$ and $\frac{\partial R}{\partial\mu} = \frac{\Gamma}{4\pi \rho_0 R^2}$) gives the **exact curvature-capped TOV equation**:

$$\frac{dp}{dR} = -\frac{\left(\rho + p/c^2\right) G}{R^2\,f_{\text{LQC}}(R, m)}\left[m\left(1 - \frac{4\bar{\rho}}{\rho_{\max}}\right) + \frac{4\pi R^3 p}{c^2}\left(1 - \frac{2\bar{\rho}}{\rho_{\max}}\right)\right].$$

   Can the shock-heated fluid settle into a static ($U = 0$) hydrostatic core with $p \ge 0$ and $p(R_{\text{surf}}) = 0$ inside the black hole ($R < R_+$)? **No:**
   - In the trapped region $R_-(M) < R < R_+(M)$ (which contains the effective-potential minimum $R_{\text{eq}} = 4^{1/3}R_{\text{core}}(M)$ where $\bar{\rho} = \frac{1}{4}\rho_{\max}$), $f_{\text{LQC}}(R, M) < 0$ (`-9.18` at $M=50$, `-15.16` at $M=100$), so any $U = 0$ state has $\Gamma^2 = f_{\text{LQC}} < 0$ (violating Lorentzian signature).
   - Inside the untrapped timelike core region $0 < R < R_-(M)$ (where $f_{\text{LQC}}(R, M) > 0$), we have $\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\rho_{\max}} > 1 - \frac{R_-(M)}{r_s} > \frac{1}{2}$ everywhere (`>= 0.953` at $M=50$, `>= 0.970` at $M=100$). Consequently, **both $1 - \frac{4\bar{\rho}}{\rho_{\max}} < -1 < 0$ and $1 - \frac{2\bar{\rho}}{\rho_{\max}} < 0$** (even the active gravitational weight of positive pressure $p > 0$ flips sign and becomes repulsive!), making $\frac{dp}{dR} > 0$ strictly for all $p \ge 0$. Pressure increases outward across $(0, R_-)$ and can never reach $p(R_{\text{surf}}) = 0$ inside $R < R_-(M)$. (Only negative vacuum tension $p = -\rho_{\max}c^2 < 0$, the V1 de Sitter phase, gives $\frac{dp}{dR} = 0$.) Thus **any positive-pressure core must dynamically bounce and re-expand**. This is a lemma under the effective equation above; the $(R_-, R_+)$ half is trivial, since no static configuration exists where $f < 0$.

2. **Dynamical Shock Heating Prevents Shell Crossing, Preserves Lorentzian Signature, & Converges at $\mathcal{O}(1/N^2)$ (Tests 35–36):**
   On identical inhomogeneous initial data ($M = 20, r_s = 40\ell, R_{\text{core}}(M) = 3.420\ell, R_-(M) = 3.527\ell, \delta = 0.42$):
   - **Pressureless dust ($p = 0$)** suffers shell crossing ($\min(R_{k+1} - R_k) = -7.11\times 10^{-3}\ell < 0$).
   - **Relativistic Einstein–Euler fluid ($\gamma = 4/3$, physical Planck dissipation length $\ell_{\text{visc}} = 0.06\ell$)** converts differential bounce kinetic energy into relativistic thermal energy ($\langle\varepsilon\rangle$ amplified $48.2\times$, $\max c_s = 0.172\,c < c$), completely prevents shell crossing ($\min\frac{\Delta R_k(\tau)}{\Delta R_k(0)} = 0.0881 > 0$), conserves $M_{\text{ADM}}$ to `0.00e+00`, and maintains a strictly Lorentzian metric ($\min\Gamma = 0.783 > 0$, $|\Gamma^2 - f_{\text{LQC}} - U^2/c^2| \le 2.37\times 10^{-8}$).
   - **Continuum Convergence ($\Delta\mu \to 0, N = 30 \to 60 \to 120 \to 240$ at fixed $\ell = 1$ and fixed $\ell_{\text{visc}} = 0.06\ell$, Test 36):** The half-mass trajectory $L_2$ error vs. $N = 240$ converges at second order $\mathcal{O}(1/N^2)$ (`1.14e-5 -> 2.73e-6 -> 5.80e-7`, empirical order $p = 2.15$), while $\min(\Delta R_k/\Delta R_{k,0})$ (`0.08648 -> 0.08775 -> 0.08812 -> 0.08825`, `rel_err = 1.44e-3`) and $\langle\varepsilon\rangle_{\max}$ (`0.02570 -> 0.02447 -> 0.02409 -> 0.02400`, `rel_err = 3.51e-3`) converge monotonically at $\mathcal{O}(1/N^2)$.
   - **Dependence on the dissipation length (`viscosity_sweep_audit.py`):** Because $\ell_{\text{visc}}$ is fixed in physical units, $\mathcal{O}(1/N^2)$ convergence is convergence to the *viscous* PDE, not to an inviscid limit. At $N = 120$, $\ell_{\text{visc}} = 0.03, 0.06, 0.12\ell$ gives core heating $21\times, 48\times, 129\times$, $\min\Gamma = 0.83, 0.78, 0.53$ and $\min\Delta R = 0.0059, 0.0088, 0.0123$, while $R_{\text{surf,min}}$ changes by only $1.5\%$ and every shell still turns around inside $R_-(m_i)$. The qualitative results are robust; the quoted magnitudes are $\ell_{\text{visc}}$-dependent, and at $\ell_{\text{visc}} = 0.25\ell$ (Test 37) the outcome changes from a bounce to a $\Gamma \to 0$ throat.

3. **The Misner–Sharp Trapped-Region Hyperbola Theorem & Spatial Throat Threshold (Test 37):**
   Inside the trapped region $R_-(m) < R < R_+(m)$ where $f_{\text{LQC}}(R, m) < 0$, the exact invariant $\Gamma^2 = f_{\text{LQC}}(R, m) + \frac{U^2}{c^2}$ defines the hyperbola:

$$\left(\frac{U}{c}\right)^2 - \Gamma^2 = \left|f_{\text{LQC}}(R, m)\right| > 0.$$

   Because the infalling branch ($U \le -c\sqrt{|f_{\text{LQC}}|} < 0$) and the outgoing anti-trapped branch ($U \ge +c\sqrt{|f_{\text{LQC}}|} > 0$) are separated by a forbidden gap of width $2c\sqrt{|f_{\text{LQC}}|}$, **no causal pressure force can turn a shell around ($U = 0$) in the trapped region $R > R_-(m)$**:
   - In the **causal core-bounce window** (both $\gamma = 4/3$ and $\gamma = 5/3$ with $\ell_{\text{visc}} = 0.06\ell$), every shell $i = 1, \dots, N$ falls inside its own inner horizon $R_{\text{turn},i} < R_-(m_i)$ (`R_surf_min = 3.452 < R_-(M) = 3.527` for $\gamma=4/3$, `3.469 < 3.527` for $\gamma=5/3$) where $f_{\text{LQC}}(R_{\text{turn},i}, m_i) = \Gamma_i^2 > 0$ is timelike, turns around smoothly with $\Gamma > 0$, and re-expands onto the anti-trapped ($U > 0$) white-hole branch.
   - Conversely, in the **over-pressurized trapped-region regime** ($\ell_{\text{visc}} = 0.25\ell, \delta = 0.50$), when the outward shock pressure wave from the rebounding inner core decelerates an infalling outer shell down to $|U| = c\sqrt{|f_{\text{LQC}}(R, m)|}$ while it is still in the trapped region (`R_surf_min = 3.562 > R_-(M) = 3.527`), $U$ cannot cross zero; instead, $\Gamma = \frac{\partial R}{\partial\ell_{\text{proper}}}$ is driven through zero (`min_Gamma = -0.194 < 0`), forming a **spatial throat / neck singularity ($\partial R/\partial\ell_{\text{proper}} = 0$, $g_{\mu\mu} \to \infty$)** in the trapped region.

### 4.9 3D Random Voronoi–Delaunay Lattice, Rayleigh Scattering Bounds, and Unstructured Falling-Mesh Invariance (`random_voronoi_and_mesh_audit.py`, Module 12)
In response to the question of whether a **random grid** would be closer to physical space than a cubic crystal lattice, `random_voronoi_and_mesh_audit.py` (Module 12, Tests 38–40) audits both a static 3D random Voronoi–Delaunay tessellation (**Christ, Friedberg & Lee 1982**) and a co-moving unstructured random falling mesh:

1. **Exact 3D Voronoi–Delaunay Discrete Exterior Calculus vs. Naive Random Graphs (Test 38):**
   On a 3D random point cloud, a naive distance-weighted graph Laplacian $w_{ij} \propto e^{-l_{ij}^2}$ fails at $\mathcal{O}(1/\ell)$ even on a pure linear gradient $\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x} + b$ ($\text{rms error} = 0.525$), because random neighbor vectors do not cancel at first order. ### 4.11 Non-GR Grid Mechanisms & Outcome-5 Follow-Up Checks (Module 14, Tests 48–55)

1. **Pure 3D 18-Point Hooke's-Law Vector Spring Relaxation vs. Gravity (Eshelby 1957 Inclusion Problem, Test 48):**
   Solving the exact 3-component vector displacement equilibrium $D_{ab}(\mathbf{q})u_b(\mathbf{q}) = f_a(\mathbf{q})$ on an $N = 64^3$ 3D 18-point central-force spring network (6 axial bars with stiffness $k_1$, 12 face-diagonal bars with stiffness $k_2$) proves:

We **postulate** the effective-LQC holonomy substitution $\beta^2 \to \sin^2(\ell\beta)/\ell^2$ (Ashtekar, Pawłowski & Singh 2006; Kelly, Santacruz & Wilson-Ewing 2020), motivated by bounded holonomies around a minimum-area plaquette but not derived here:
     $$H_{\text{hol}}(v, \beta) = \frac{3vc^2}{2\ell^2}\sin^2(\ell\beta) - mc^2 = 0.$$
   - Hamilton's equations, evaluated with numerical central differences of $H_{\text{hol}}$ and integrated with RK4, give $\dot{R}/R = \sin(2\ell\beta)/(2\ell)$ and hence, on shell, $(\dot{R}/R)^2 = \frac{2Gm}{R^3}\left(1 - \frac{2Gm\ell^2}{c^2R^3}\right)$: exactly the effective-LQC law imposed per shell in Module 9 (V2), verified symbolically and along the numerical trajectory (relative residual `5.2e-8`; constraint drift `2.9e-7`). This test therefore supplies the Hamiltonian origin of the Module 9 bounce law; it is a continuum ODE in $\tau$ and no lattice structure enters.
   - The turning point is reached at $\ell\beta = -\pi/2$ (`abs_err = 6.96e-4`, limited by $\Delta\tau$) with $R_{\min} = (2Gm\ell^2/c^2)^{1/3}$ (`rel_err = 1.47e-7`) and $\bar\rho = 3c^2/(8\pi G\ell^2)$ (`rel_err = 4.40e-7`), while $H_{\text{cont}}$ collapses to $v \to 0$. **Caveats:** $R_{\min}$ and $\rho_{\max}$ follow algebraically from $\sin^2 \le 1$; the integration only confirms the trajectory reaches the bound. Their values are fixed by the chosen normalisation: $4\sin^2(\ell\beta/2)/\ell^2$, which also tends to $\beta^2$ as $\ell \to 0$, gives a $4\times$ larger $\rho_{\max}$. "Bragg reflection" is an analogy: $H_{\text{hol}}$ is periodic in $\beta$ with period $\pi/\ell$, and $\dot v \propto \sin(2\ell\beta)$ vanishes at the edge of that zone.
7. **Minisuperspace Einstein–Hilbert + Schutz Fluid Action: de Sitter Region and the 1916 Interior Star (Test 54):**
   - The same discretised minisuperspace Einstein–Hilbert action as Test 52 (not simplicial Regge) is coupled to matter through $\kappa\sqrt{-g}\,p$ (Schutz 1970, pressure as Lagrangian): $-\kappa R^2\rho_{\max}a\mathcal{A}$ for a de Sitter region ($p = -\rho_{\max}c^2$), and $\kappa\rho_0 R^2\mathcal{A}(a_{\text{surf}} - a)$ for an incompressible fluid, i.e. $p(a) = \rho_0c^2(a_{\text{surf}}/a - 1)$ (Tolman redshift of the chemical potential; the equation of state is an input). Symbolically, the Euler–Lagrange equations of the total action are exactly $-R^2\mathcal{A}\,(G^t{}_t + \kappa\rho)$ and $-R^2a\,(G^r{}_r - \kappa p)$, so the stationary points are the Einstein/TOV equations by construction. Solving $\partial S/\partial a_s = 0$, $\partial S/\partial\mathcal{A}_v = 0$ numerically converges at $\mathcal{O}(1/N^2)$ across $N = 50 \to 100 \to 200$ to:
     1. **de Sitter region ($\rho = \rho_{\max}$, $R \le 0.8\ell$):** $\mathcal{A}(R) = (1 - R^2/\ell^2)^{-1/2}$ (`err_A = 1.01e-5`, $p_{\mathcal{A}} = 2.014$) and $a(R) = \sqrt{1 - R^2/\ell^2}$ up to a constant time rescaling (`err_a = 6.18e-6`, $p_a = 1.991$). The static patch ends at $R = \ell$ (the Hayward inner horizon $r_- \approx \ell$), so this resolves a region smaller than one lattice spacing ($\Delta R = 0.004\ell$). It is a continuum-limit check, not Planck-lattice physics, and not a static core of size $R_{\text{core}} \gg \ell$ (none exists; Test 34).
     2. **Karl Schwarzschild's (1916) constant-density interior ($2GM/(c^2R_{\text{star}}) = 0.5$):** with only $a(R_{\text{star}}) = a_{\text{surf}}$ imposed from exterior matching, $\mathcal{A}(R)$ (`err_A = 5.40e-6`, $p_{\mathcal{A}} = 2.006$), $a(R) = \frac{3}{2}\sqrt{1 - r_s/R_{\text{star}}} - \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1 - r_s R^2/R_{\text{star}}^3}$ (`err_a = 1.07e-5`, $p_a = 1.998$), and $p(R)$ (`err_p = 7.35e-5`, $p_p = 1.998$). An earlier version seeded the outer shell with the exact interior solution; that has been removed.
8. **3D Periodic Kuhn Regge Complex: Flat-Space Gauge Zero Modes and Static TT Curvature (Test 55):**
   - A periodic Kuhn triangulation of an $L^3$ torus ($L = 4$: $64$ vertices, $384$ tetrahedra, $448$ edges = $192$ axial, $192$ face-diagonal, $64$ body-diagonal) is evaluated with the 3D Regge action $S_{\text{Regge}}^{3\text{D}} = \sum_e \ell_e\,\delta_e$ via Cayley–Menger dihedral angles. **This complex is not the 18-point stencil:** each vertex has degree $14$ (6 axial, only 6 of the 12 face diagonals, and 2 of the 8 body diagonals), and the triangulation singles out the $(1,1,1)$ direction. $S_{\text{Regge}}^{3\text{D}}$ is 3D Euclidean Regge gravity $= \tfrac12\int\sqrt{g}\,{}^{(3)}R$, the spatial-curvature term of the ADM Hamiltonian: there is no time direction, and 3D gravity has no propagating gravitons.
     1. **Flat-space gauge zero modes, broken on curved backgrounds:** random vertex displacements (rms $0.03\ell$ per component) change Hooke's energy by `0.538`, but because the displaced tetrahedra still tile flat $\mathbb{R}^3$ every deficit stays zero (`3.55e-15`) and $S$ is unchanged (`1.18e-13`) at finite amplitude. On a curved background ($L = 8$, TT amplitude $0.08$) the same kind of displacement changes $S$ by `6.3e-6` ($\sigma = 0.01$) and `1.6e-5` ($\sigma = 0.02$), so the exact symmetry holds only around flat space (the known broken discrete diffeomorphisms of Regge calculus; Bahr & Dittrich 2009). The vanishing Regge vertex force (`6.5e-9` vs. Hooke `0.406`) follows from flatness, since $\partial S/\partial\ell_e = \delta_e = 0$ for every edge variation at a flat configuration (Schläfli). It is not independent evidence of gauge invariance.
     2. **Static TT curvature reproduces the continuum action:** for $g_{ij}$ with $h_{xy}(z) = \epsilon\cos(2\pi z/L)$ (unimodular), deficits become non-zero (`0.0338 rad` at $\epsilon = 0.04$), $\Delta S(2\epsilon)/\Delta S(\epsilon) = 4.0025$, and $\Delta S_{\text{Regge}}$ ($-0.0300$ at $L = 4$) approaches the Fierz–Pauli value $\tfrac12\int\sqrt{g}R = -\tfrac18\epsilon^2k^2L^3$ with ratio $0.950, 0.987, 0.997$ at $L = 4, 8, 16$ (convergence order $2.0$ in $k\ell$). This is the genuine quantitative result: the simplicial Regge action on this complex converges to the continuum Einstein–Hilbert spatial curvature action for a TT deformation. It is a static check, not gravitational-wave dynamics.

### 4.12 Linearised Lorentzian (3+1D, Time-Dependent) Regge Calculus on a 4D Lattice (Module 15, Tests 56–58)
In `lorentzian_regge_4d.py` (`tests/module_15_lorentzian_regge_4d.py`) the lattice model is defined by two postulates only:
- **Geometry:** spacetime is a periodic Freudenthal (Kuhn) triangulation of a hypercubic lattice. Each hypercube is split into 24 flat Lorentzian 4-simplices (monotone lattice paths), giving 15 edge directions $d \in \{0,1\}^4\setminus 0$ per vertex. The background is flat with $G = \mathrm{diag}(-\alpha^2, 1, 1, 1)$, i.e. $\Delta t = \alpha\,\Delta x$; with $\alpha < 1$ the only timelike edge is $(1,0,0,0)$, and there are no null edges or null triangles.
- **Dynamics:** the Regge action $S = \sum_t |A_t|\,\varepsilon_t$ with $\varepsilon_t = -\sum_{\sigma \ni t} c^\sigma_t\,\theta^\sigma_t + \text{const}$ (Regge 1961; Sorkin 1975).

Nothing else is input: no Einstein equations, no metric field, no graviton, no speed of propagation. Each simplex's areas (Heron), rotation angles at timelike triangles and boost angles at spacelike triangles are computed from its 10 squared edge lengths through the Gram matrix of edge vectors and its inverse (inward facet normals). Derivatives are taken by complex-step differentiation, exact to round-off. At flat space the second variation is $H_{ef} = -\sum_\sigma\sum_{t\in\sigma} c^\sigma_t\,(\partial|A_t|/\partial s_e)(\partial\theta^\sigma_t/\partial s_f)$, Fourier-transformed to a $15\times15$ matrix $H(k)$.

1. **Hinge signs, gauge invariance and the Bianchi identity (Test 56).** The relative weights $c^\sigma_t$ are not chosen by hand. They are the left null vector of $|A_t|\,\partial\theta_t/\partial s_e$, i.e. the Lorentzian Schläfli identity $\sum_t c_t|A_t|\,d\theta_t = 0$ of each flat simplex (residual `2.0e-17`). The result is $c = +1$ on all 72 timelike hinge slots, $+1$ on 24 and $-1$ on 144 spacelike slots (the signs reflect the boost-angle branch), with $\big||c|-1\big| < 2\times10^{-14}$; the Euclidean control gives $c \equiv 1$. Over 20 random $k$: $H(k)$ is Hermitian (`3.8e-15`), annihilates the 4 vertex-translation (discrete diffeomorphism) vectors $\delta s_d = 2\langle d, \xi\rangle_G(e^{ik\cdot d} - 1)$ (`5.1e-16`), satisfies the contracted Bianchi identity $g^\dagger H = 0$ (`8.2e-16`), and the same vectors leave all 50 linearised deficit angles unchanged (`4.5e-16`). **Lattice artefact:** $H$ has a 5th exact zero mode at every $k$, because the hyperdiagonal edge $(1,1,1,1)$ drops out of the quadratic action identically (its Hessian column vanishes to `1e-15`), even though it changes deficit angles. It is non-propagating and decoupled, and it is fixed by hand like a gauge choice.
2. **The graviton from the lattice action (Test 57).** Real solutions $e^{i(k_t n + \mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x})}$ exist where the gauge-reduced Hessian acquires extra null vectors. Counting negative eigenvalues of $Q^\dagger H Q$ over the **entire** temporal zone $k_t \in (0, \pi]$, for 4 spatial directions, 4 wavenumbers and $\alpha = 0.5, 0.3$, there is **exactly one crossing and it is doubly degenerate**: two polarisations and no other propagating mode. Its physical speed $v = k_t/(\alpha|\mathbf{k}|)$ obeys $1 - v/c = \mathcal{O}(k^2)$ with measured orders $2.000 \pm 0.001$ ($1.96\times10^{-5}$ along $z$ at $|\mathbf{k}| = 0.025$, $\alpha = 0.5$), independently of $\alpha$ and direction. Both null vectors are transverse-traceless: after removing lattice gauge and the hyperdiagonal, their metric content (fitted through $\delta s_d = d^\mu d^\nu h_{\mu\nu}$) has TT fraction $> 1 - 2.1\times10^{-9}$ for $\mathbf{k} \parallel z$, and their non-metric remainder is $< 4\times10^{-5}$. The Euclidean-signature control has no real propagating modes, as an elliptic theory should not.
3. **Discrete-time Lorentzian Regge evolution (Test 58).** The equations $E_n = A_{-1}s_{n-1} + A_0 s_n + A_{+1}s_{n+1} = 0$ (one per edge owned by slice $n$) are solved slice by slice for each spatial Fourier mode. The unknowns per step are 3 slice-connecting edges, 6 spatial edges of the next slice and the lagged spatial body diagonal, after fixing the 4 lapse/shift edges and the hyperdiagonal. That is 10 unknowns against 15 equations, 4 of which are Bianchi-redundant constraints and 1 of which vanishes identically. Results:
   - Exact on-shell plane waves propagate for 2000 steps with error `9.9e-8` and equation residual `3.0e-13`.
   - The one-step transfer map has no eigenvalue with $|\lambda| > 1$ ($\max|\lambda| - 1 = 5\times10^{-11}$). Every unit-modulus eigenvalue is a graviton ($k_t = \pm k_t^{\text{graviton}}$) or a residual gauge mode ($k_t \in \{0, -k_x, -k_y, -k_z\}$). Constraint-violating perturbations grow at most about linearly ($\|T^{4000}\|/\|T^{1000}\| = 2.8$), the usual behaviour of free evolution without constraint damping. On top of an exact solution a `1e-12` perturbation reaches `2e-5` after 20,000 steps at $|\mathbf{k}| = 0.01$ and stays below `1e-6` at $|\mathbf{k}| = 0.2$.
   - A real-space cross-polarised graviton packet ($k_0 = 0.2$, 1200 steps, $N_z = 1024$) keeps its norm to `3e-10` and moves at $0.99622\,c$, matching the lattice dispersion prediction $0.99624\,c$.

**What this does and does not establish.** Within the stated postulates, the linearised Einstein dynamics follow from the 4D Lorentzian lattice action with no further input: diffeomorphism gauge symmetry, the Bianchi identity, exactly two transverse-traceless degrees of freedom propagating at $c$, and causal discrete-time evolution. This is the lattice counterpart of the Fierz–Pauli limit established for Euclidean Regge calculus by Roček & Williams (1981, 1984). It is **not** a derivation of full nonlinear GR. Nonlinear 3+1D Regge evolution (where discrete diffeomorphisms are broken on curved backgrounds), coupling to matter and black-hole spacetimes are not done. The Regge action is itself the discrete Einstein–Hilbert action, so the result shows that GR's linear dynamics emerge from that lattice postulate, not from a more primitive non-gravitational rule. The Freudenthal lattice is also not the 18-point stencil of Sections 1–4.

### 4.13 Quantum aspects
Promoting $(\Phi_i, \Pi_i)$ to operators gives linear Heisenberg evolution that preserves $[\hat\Phi_i, \hat\Pi_j] = i\hbar\delta_{ij}$ (drift $3.8\times10^{-14}$ over 200 steps) and satisfies Ehrenfest's theorem trivially, because the system is linear (so no Ehrenfest check is counted). The lattice ground state has a finite entanglement entropy across a cut ($S_{\text{ent}} = 1.11$ for the 60-cell test chain). None of this extends to the saturation step, which is non-unitary, and Section 4.5 shows that the core cannot hold the black hole's information. Gravitationally induced entanglement (Bose–Marletto–Vedral) is predicted by any quantised gravitational interaction and is not a prediction specific to this model, which in any case contains no interaction between two masses.

### 5.3 Predictions and What Modules 11–13 Tell Us Next
At present the model makes **no unconditional, distinctive observational prediction**, while **Modules 12–13** establish sharp ultraviolet and structural constraints on how a discrete lattice can represent General Relativity:
1. Under the **HKSW (2022) connection shock law** (Module 10, Tests 30–31), the black hole transitions into an outgoing shock wave that exits $r_+$ in time $\Delta t_{\text{shock}}(M) = \frac{8\pi G^2 M^2}{3c^5\ell} - \frac{8GM}{3c^3} - \frac{\pi\ell}{3c} \propto M^2$, which would predict explosive white-hole bursts for primordial black holes of mass $M \sim \left(\frac{3c^5\ell\,t_0}{8\pi G^2}\right)^{1/2} \approx 2\times10^{25}\text{ g}$. **However**, Test 31 proves that this shock is spacelike (superluminal relative to the exterior metric) throughout $[R_-, R_+]$.
2. Under the **causal general-relativistic Misner–Sharp / Einstein–Euler fluid equations** (Module 11, Tests 34–37), converting the bounce kinetic energy into relativistic thermal shock pressure $p = (\gamma - 1)\rho_0\varepsilon > 0$ completely resolves both the shell-crossing caustic of inviscid dust and the Lorentzian signature breakdown of cold sticky dust:
   - By the **Curvature-Capped TOV Theorem** (Test 34), the shock-heated fluid **cannot** remain trapped in a static positive-pressure core inside $R_-(M)$, because $\frac{dp}{dR} > 0$ strictly for all $p \ge 0$ inside $R < R_-(M)$.
   - By the **Trapped-Region Hyperbola Theorem** (Tests 35–37), in the causal core-bounce window every shell falls inside $R \le R_-(m_i) \approx R_{\text{core}}(m_i)$, bounces smoothly with $\Gamma > 0$ and $\partial_\mu R > 0$, and re-expands onto the **anti-trapped ($U > 0$) white-hole branch** of the Misner–Sharp hyperbola $(U/c)^2 - \Gamma^2 = |f_{\text{LQC}}(R, m)|$, whereas excessive shock pressure in the trapped region $R > R_-(m_i)$ drives a **spatial throat ($\Gamma = \partial R/\partial\ell_{\text{proper}} \to 0$, baby-universe / bag-of-gold neck)**.
   - Determining whether the anti-trapped ($U > 0$) re-expanding core emerges into the original exterior universe (black-to-white-hole transition; Haggard & Rovelli 2015; Lewandowski et al. 2023) or into a second asymptotic region across the horizon chart discontinuity (Fazzini, Rovelli & Soltani 2023) is a global spacetime-matching question that cannot be decided by local comoving PDEs alone.
3. **PeV Gamma-Ray Constraint on Static Random Spatial Lattices (Module 12, Test 39):**
   - A static 3D random Voronoi–Delaunay lattice passes the Fermi-LAT GRB 090510 test at $31\text{ GeV}$ ($\tau = 8.3\times10^{-14}$), but if it is the physical spacetime its shear disorder ($\sigma_{\text{shear}} = 0.0765$) gives $\tau \approx 2.2$ for $2.5\text{ PeV}$ photons over the $\sim1.4$ kpc LHAASO baseline. Static random spatial lattices are therefore disfavoured; periodic crystals ($\tau = 0$) and co-moving random meshes (Test 40) are not affected.
4. **Structural Requirements for Anamorphic Discretisation & 3D Spin-2 Lattice Gravity (Module 13, Tests 43–47):**
   - Discretising Einstein's spherical constraint equations in anamorphic variables reproduces the Schwarzschild exterior and interior and the PG river from $\rho(R)$ at $\mathcal{O}(1/N^2)$ without a metric ansatz, but with Einstein's equations as input. In 3D, representing all six strain components requires at least the 18-point bar network (rank 6 vs. rank 3). Deriving the dynamics (Bianchi identity, spin-2 couplings) would require a lattice action such as Regge calculus; that is not done here.

## 6. Limitations and References

### 6.1 Known limitations
1. **Dynamical geometry beyond spherical symmetry and linearized 3D spin-2 perturbations.** While Sections 4.6–4.9 solve the spherically symmetric LTB, post-bounce shock, and anisotropic Misner–Sharp / Einstein–Euler equations and Section 4.10 (Module 13, Tests 43–47) discretises the static/river constraint equations from $\rho(R)$ and evolves the 6-component linearized TT tensor $h_{ij}^{\text{TT}}$ as decoupled scalar waves, the 3D "self-gravity" update in Section 4.4 is an ad-hoc reservoir rule rather than full nonlinear 3+1D Baumgarte–Shapiro–Shibata–Nakamura (BSSN) or Regge evolution.
2. **Test 23 scalar toy vs. Module 13 Spin-2 sector.** Test 23 (Module 8) was a two-component scalar toy with an ad-hoc coupling. **Module 13 (Tests 46–47)** treats the full 6-component symmetric $3\times 3$ perturbation $h_{ij}(\mathbf{x}, t)$ on the 18-point lattice, but only as six decoupled scalar waves in TT gauge (whose TT and Bianchi identities hold by construction), and evaluates the continuum Regge–Wheeler formula with the spin factor $1 - s^2 = -3$ taken as input. No lattice action generates the spin-2 dynamics, and nonlinear backreaction is not evolved.
3. **Resolution.** The 3D runs resolve $r_s$ with ~3 cells and do not resolve $r_-$; they last $1.65\,t_P$ on a periodic box.
4. **Rotation.** The rotating flow is ad hoc. Kerr cannot be written in the flat-slice river form used here; it does not even admit axisymmetric conformally flat slices (Garat & Price 2000).
5. **Lorentz invariance.** A fundamental cubic lattice faces the Collins–Perez–Sudarsky–Urrutia–Vucetich loop problem; only tree-level dispersion is controlled here.
6. **Saturation rule.** Non-symplectic, non-unitary, and its physical content (what the reservoir is) is only consistent if the reservoir is vacuum-like matter.
7. **Planck-scale tidal forces.** Tidal terms are bounded but of order $c^2/\ell_P^2 \sim 10^{86}\,\text{s}^{-2}$; "bounded" does not mean survivable.
8. **1D inner-horizon test.** Test 13 uses a planar (1+1) wave equation on the Hayward river profile, without the $r^2$ factors and angular-momentum potential of a true spherical wave. It shows the mechanism, not precise numbers.
9. **Choices in the postulates.** The factor $\frac12$ in $E_{\max}$, the identification $\ell = \ell_P$, and the per-cell use of the Bekenstein bound are choices or heuristics, not derived results.
10. **Dust approximation at shell crossing.** Module 10 shows that continuing pressureless dust ($p = 0$) past the bounce shock either requires a spacelike (superluminal) shock inside $[R_-, R_+]$ (HKSW 2022) or violates the LTB Lorentzian signature bound $1 + 2e_{\text{mech}}/c^2 > 0$ under inelastic shell coalescence.
11. **Global exterior matching of the anti-trapped ($U > 0$) Einstein–Euler bounce.** Module 11 solves the comoving Lagrangian Misner–Sharp / Einstein–Euler equations through the bounce and into the anti-trapped ($U > 0$) white-hole branch, and proves that over-deceleration in the trapped region $R > R_-(m)$ forms a $\Gamma = \partial R/\partial\ell_{\text{proper}} \to 0$ spatial throat. How the anti-trapped ($U > 0$) interior matches globally onto an exterior asymptotic chart requires global junction/tunneling conditions beyond a single Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinate patch (Fazzini, Rovelli & Soltani 2023).

12. **No lattice-generated dynamics.** Module 9 integrates independent per-shell ODEs, and Modules 10–13 discretise continuum Einstein / effective-LQC equations; none of them derives gravitational dynamics from a microscopic lattice rule. In Module 14, Test 53 is the continuum effective-LQC Hamiltonian with a postulated polymerisation, Tests 52 and 54 vary a minisuperspace Einstein–Hilbert action, and Test 55 is static 3D Euclidean Regge calculus; no 3+1D lattice evolution is performed. Module 15 performs genuine discrete-time Lorentzian Regge evolution, but only of the linearised equations on a flat background.
13. **Viscosity dependence.** The Module 11–12 shock results converge to a viscous PDE whose solution depends on $\ell_{\text{visc}}$ (heating $21\times$–$129\times$ for $\ell_{\text{visc}} = 0.03$–$0.12\ell$; a throat instead of a bounce at $0.25\ell$).
14. **Preferred frame.** A fundamental cubic lattice, or a co-moving PG/LTB foliation treated as physical, selects a frame (cf. Einstein-aether and Hořava gravity), which generically adds a scalar mode; this sector is not analysed. The Fermi-LAT comparison is against a linear ($n = 1$) LIV model, which quadratic tree-level dispersion passes trivially.
