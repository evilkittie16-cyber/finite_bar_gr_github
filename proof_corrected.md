## 1. Curvature Breakdown in Continuous GR & The Planck-Scale UV Lattice Postulates

In standard continuous General Relativity, spacetime is modeled as a smooth manifold divisible down to zero-volume points ($r \to 0, \ell = 0$).

$$\frac{2G\,M_{\text{sharp}}(r)}{c^2\,r} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{r_s}{r} & \text{for } r \ge R_{\text{core}}(M) \quad (\text{exact Schwarzschild GR outside the core}) \\[10pt] \dfrac{r_s}{R_{\text{core}}(M)^3}\,r^2 = \dfrac{r^2}{\ell^2} & \text{for } r < R_{\text{core}}(M) \quad (\text{regular de Sitter core—bounded curvature at } r = 0) \end{cases}$$

$$G^t_t = G^r_r = \frac{r f'(r) + f(r) - 1}{r^2} = -\frac{3r_s}{R_{\text{core}}^3} = -\frac{8\pi G}{c^2}\rho_{\max} \implies p_r = -\rho_{\max}c^2$$

$$\rho_{\max}c^2 + p_r + 2p_\theta = -2\rho_{\max}c^2 < 0$$

2. **At & Inside the Event Horizon Down to the Core Surface ($R_{\text{core}}(M) \le r \le r_+$):** At the outer horizon $r_+$, $\lvert J(r_+)\rvert = c$. Inside ($R_{\text{core}}(M) \le r < r_+$), $\lvert J(r)\rvert > c$: **space and time swap roles** so that moving forward in time carries all waves and matter inward toward the core surface $r = R_{\text{core}}(M)$, where the flow reaches its peak supersonic regime ($r_{\text{peak}} = 2^{1/3}R_{\text{core}}(M)$):
   $$\left.\frac{J(r)^2}{c^2}\right\vert_{r = R_{\text{core}}(M)} = \left(\frac{r_s}{\ell}\right)^{2/3} > 1 \quad (\text{sharp core}) \qquad\text{or}\qquad \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{r_s}{\ell}\right)^{2/3} > 1 \quad (\text{smooth Hayward for } r_s > 2\sqrt{2}\ell)$$
3. **Inside the Finite Core ($r < R_{\text{core}}(M)$) & How Cell Freezing Excises the Inner Cauchy Horizon ($r_- \approx \ell$):** Deep inside the core ($r \ll R_{\text{core}}(M)$), $\frac{J(r)^2}{c^2} \approx \frac{r_s r^2}{r_s\ell^2} = \frac{r^2}{\ell^2}$ slows back down to $c$ at the inner Cauchy horizon scale $r_- \approx \ell$ and reaches **$J(0) = 0$** at dead center.
   - **Why Continuous Regular Black Holes Suffer Mass Inflation at $r_- \approx \ell$ vs. Why Our Frozen Core Excises It:** In a continuous fluid model without cell freezing (**Hayward 2006; Visser et al. 2018–2024**), infalling waves pass unimpeded through $r = R_{\text{core}}(M)$ all the way down to the inner Cauchy horizon at $r_- \approx \ell$, where $\lvert J(r_-)\rvert = c$ decelerates back to light speed and causes infinite wave pile-up (*mass inflation*). In our discrete model, all cells inside $r \le R_{\text{core}}(M) = (r_s\ell^2)^{1/3} \gg \ell$ are **already saturated at $\rho_{\max}$ and frozen solid** ($\dot{\Phi}_i = 0, K_{ij} = 0, M_{ij} = 0$). ### 4.2 Exact Energy-Conserving 3D Grid Hamiltonian (Fixed-Background Wave Evolution)
On the 3D cubic lattice of spacing $\ell$, let $\Phi_i$ be the wave amplitude and $\Pi_i$ be its conjugate momentum in cell $i$ at position $\mathbf{x}_i$, with 3D radial river velocity vector $\mathbf{J}_i = J(r_i)\,\hat{\mathbf{r}}_i$. On a fixed gravitational background ($M = \text{const}$), the wave energy is governed by the canonical Hamiltonian:

$$H_{\text{wave}}(\Phi, \Pi) = \sum_i \frac{\Pi_i^2}{2a_i} + \sum_{i,j} \Pi_i M_{ij}\Phi_j + \frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j} K_{ij}(\Phi_i - \Phi_j)^2$$

where:
- $a_i > 0$ is the cell inertia weight and $K_{ij} = K_{ji} = \frac{K_0}{\ell^2}W_{\mathbf{d}} > 0$ is the symmetric spring coupling across the 18-point stencil ($\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{x}_j - \mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{N}_{18}$),
- $M_{ij}$ is the **3D 18-point skew-symmetric (summation-by-parts) discrete river advection operator** with units of $[\text{Time}^{-1}] = [J/\ell]$:

$$M_{ij} = \frac{1}{4\ell}\sum_{\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{N}_{18}} W_d\,\frac{(\mathbf{J}_i + \mathbf{J}_j)\cdot(\mathbf{d}/\ell)}{2}\left(\delta_{j,i+\mathbf{d}} - \delta_{j,i-\mathbf{d}}\right) = -M_{ji}$$

  *(Note on normalization, $\sqrt{-g}=1$ in Cartesian PG coordinates, and symmetric acoustic divergence: because the 18- and 27-point stencil second moment is $\sum_{\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{N}} W_d\left(d_\alpha/\ell\right)^2 = 2$ and summing over both $\pm\mathbf{d}$ contributes another factor of $2$, the prefactor $\frac{1}{4\ell}\cdot(2\times 2) = \frac{1}{\ell}$ gives the exact unit-normalized continuum advection operator. In 3D Cartesian Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates where $\sqrt{-g} = 1$, the minimally coupled wave equation $\partial_t\Pi_{\text{min}} - \nabla\cdot(\mathbf{J}\Pi_{\text{min}}) - c^2\nabla^2\phi = 0$ with $\Pi_{\text{min}} = \partial_t\phi - \mathbf{J}\cdot\nabla\phi$ pairs the asymmetric forward advection operator $M_{\text{forward}} = \mathbf{J}\cdot\nabla$ on $\phi$ with its continuum formal adjoint $-M_{\text{forward}}^\dagger = \nabla\cdot(\mathbf{J}\,\cdot\,)$ on $\Pi_{\text{min}}$. By using the skew-symmetric midpoint operator $\sum_j M_{ij}\Phi_j = \frac{1}{2}[\mathbf{J}\cdot\nabla\Phi + \nabla\cdot(\mathbf{J}\Phi)]_i + \mathcal{O}(\ell^2) = (\mathbf{J}\cdot\nabla\Phi + \frac{1}{2}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J})\Phi)_i + \mathcal{O}(\ell^2)$ for **both** $\Phi$ and $\Pi$, the volume-compression factor $\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_r J + \frac{2J}{r}\right)$ is distributed symmetrically between $\Phi$ and $\Pi$—corresponding to the self-adjoint Liouville-rescaled scalar field, which differs from the bare minimally coupled scalar field only by an $\mathcal{O}((\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J})^2 + \mathbf{J}\cdot\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}))$ effective curvature-potential coupling that vanishes in the geometric-optics / eikonal limit $k \gg 1/r$ and leaves horizon trapping, redshift, and dispersion identical!)*

Hamilton's equations of motion on the 3D grid are:

$$\dot{\Phi}_i = \frac{\partial H_{\text{wave}}}{\partial\Pi_i} = \frac{\Pi_i}{a_i} + \sum_j M_{ij}\Phi_j, \qquad \dot{\Pi}_i = -\frac{\partial H_{\text{wave}}}{\partial\Phi_i} = -\sum_j K_{ij}(\Phi_i - \Phi_j) - \sum_j M_{ji}\Pi_j$$

Because the shift term in $\dot{\Pi}$ uses the exact matrix transpose $-M^T$ ($M_{ji} = -M_{ij}$), on any static background slice ($\dot{a}_i = 0, \dot{K}_{ij} = 0, \dot{M}_{ij} = 0$) the rate of change of wave energy is identically zero at every time step:

$$\frac{dH_{\text{wave}}}{dt} = \sum_i \left(\frac{\partial H_{\text{wave}}}{\partial\Phi_i}\dot{\Phi}_i + \frac{\partial H_{\text{wave}}}{\partial\Pi_i}\dot{\Pi}_i\right) = \sum_i \left(-\dot{\Pi}_i\dot{\Phi}_i + \dot{\Phi}_i\dot{\Pi}_i\right) \equiv 0$$

- **Exact Symplectic Discrete-Time Evolution for Non-Separable $H_{\text{wave}}$ ($\Delta t > 0$):** Because $H_{\text{wave}}(\Phi, \Pi)$ contains the momentum-position cross-term $\sum_{i,j}\Pi_i M_{ij}\Phi_j$, it is non-separable ($H \neq T(\Pi) + V(\Phi)$). To preserve both energy and the canonical symplectic form $\Omega = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & I \\ -I & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ across discrete time ticks $\Delta t > 0$ without secular drift, discrete time evolution uses the **implicit symplectic midpoint (Cayley / Crank–Nicolson) propagator** on the phase-space state $\mathbf{z} = (\Phi, \Pi)^T$:

$$\mathbf{z}(t + \Delta t) = S(\Delta t)\,\mathbf{z}(t), \qquad S(\Delta t) = \left(I - \frac{\Delta t}{2}F\right)^{-1}\left(I + \frac{\Delta t}{2}F\right), \qquad F = \begin{pmatrix} M & A^{-1} \\ -L_K & -M^T \end{pmatrix}$$

  Because $F^T\Omega + \Omega F = 0$, the Cayley transform satisfies $S(\Delta t)^T \Omega\, S(\Delta t) \equiv \Omega$ and $H_{\text{wave}}(\mathbf{z}(t + \Delta t)) \equiv H_{\text{wave}}(\mathbf{z}(t))$ unconditionally for any $\Delta t > 0$ (`drift = 9.17e-15` over 200 horizon-crossing steps in Benchmark 4).
